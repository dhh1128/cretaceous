# CRETACEOUS — ACT 1 CONTEXT (The Failing World)

Beats 1–4, scenes 1.1 through 4.3. Days 1–2. **The whole act happens in about thirty-six hours**, from an evening in the Vitarium to the following pre-dawn outside the border-tangle — which is the pacing fact most easily lost, and the reason the crisis has to feel like a fuse rather than a season.

*This file replaces an earlier `act-1-context.md` that actually contained an Act 2 packet. Its unique material — the Act 1 recap and the Chekhov's-guns list — now lives at the head of `act-2-context.md`.*

---

## I. WHAT THIS ACT HAS TO ACCOMPLISH

**A-story:** establish the knowledge death, prove the adults will not act, and get three teenagers out through the perimeter with two stolen suits.

**B-story:** assemble the trio out of three incompatible wounds, and make the reader believe each of them would go.

**And the load nobody has been carrying:** Act 1 is where the parent-nuance arc is won or lost. The three are away from every adult for the entire middle of the book, so **all the evidence the reader will use to revise their judgment of the adults has to be planted here.** By beat 14 the reader should have known for four hundred pages what Teva only realizes in the Council chamber. Right now the act asserts that the adults are broken and offers little that quietly contradicts it. See §IV.

## II. BEAT BY BEAT

### BEAT 1 — Cold Open: The Catalyst (1.1, Teva, evening, Day 1)

The Vitarium. Cultures dimming, Omya failing a public test of the synthesis protocol, Rano pleading, elders watching from the shadows and doing nothing. Elana's yazhi Alira dies of an infection that a working immunity vector would have stopped.

*Emotional target:* not grief — **rage**. Teva's mother died of the same thing two years ago, after a Seeding that didn't take. The scene is an echo, and Teva recognizes it as one.

*Established:* the Vitarium's color diagnostics (rust-red is T-cell activation, blue is lysine/digestive) · protein-gel keys that dissolve outside the chamber's humidity · Omya as last Master Keeper · Rano knowing eighty percent of a process where eighty percent is worth nothing · Alira named, therefore a person, therefore a countable death.

### BEAT 2 — Unsettled Normalcy (2.1 Keo, 2.2 Benal, 2.3 Teva, morning, Day 2)

Not a flashback. The next morning, three attempts to carry on, each poisoned by what happened.

Keo trains Noli and masks anxiety with bravado. Benal works his mathematics and his father Marek dismisses it — *"Still staring at those dead symbols? Do something real."* Teva drills yazhi on croc protocols with a cold, useless anger.

*Emotional target:* dramatic irony. Every ordinary thing is now visibly on a clock.

*Underfunded:* this is the natural home for the charm the book currently lacks — Noli being funny, the three being young, something worth losing. See §V.

### BEAT 3 — The Debate, public and private (3.1, 3.2, 3.3, Keo, evening/night, Day 2)

3.1: the Council. Marek argues the protocols are at Genesis and they are starving beside a full basket. Hesh answers with the First Walk — two hundred left Genesis, ninety-four arrived, three years of it, with rifles and plasma cutters. Sila answers with doctrine: better to die with a clean soul than live as contaminants. Elana walks in and ends the argument without winning it. The Council does nothing.

3.2: Teva tells Keo she is going. It is not a request. He goes because he cannot let her go alone.

3.3: Benal arrives, shaking, and insists. He is terrified of everything outside the tangle and he argues his way in anyway — *"You're the delivery system. I'm the key."* Keo's response is the act's turn: not pity. Awe.

*Emotional target:* the generational split made structural — paralysed adults in one room, decisive children in another, and the reader unsure yet who is right.

### BEAT 4 — Break Into Two: The Theft (4.1, 4.2, 4.3, Keo, late night to pre-dawn, Day 2)

Keo steals food from his sleeping parents, then two suits from the Repository his parents are custodians of. He passes the taboo shelf — metal spears, shock-prods, the machete bought with two lives — and takes only bio-safe weapons. He deliberately leaves the third suit on the rack. They gel up against the pheromone barrier, slip the watcher, and go through the gap into fog and a razortail pack.

*Emotional target:* guilt, specifically. Not fear of the jungle — **shame at destroying his parents' standing**. The theft has to cost the parents, or the act has no consequence: Joram and Riel are implicated and confined, which is why no skilled rescue party ever follows.

## III. STATE AT THE ACT BOUNDARY

Everything below is the entry state for Act 2 and must match `prompts/decision-logs.md`.

**Clock:** pre-dawn, Day 2 into Day 3. **Position:** 50–100 m beyond the perimeter, in ground fog, visibility about three meters. **Immediate situation:** a razortail pack clicking nearby.

**Keo** — unsuited by choice. Tough-woven tunic and leggings, hadrosaur-hide boots with pressed-bark soles. Croc-spike, tooth-knife, fieldpack with the group's supplies, fiber-cord, spare water skin. Three strips of journey-pan, two handfuls of roasted crawlers. Septic gel on exposed skin. A leaden headache from holding Noli.

**Teva** — suited, small size, biofilm scarred but functional, thermal system green, chromatophores active. Croc-spike, knife. Same food.

**Benal** — suited, large size, **one shoulder capacitor red-lined and the shock grid compromised**. Razor-club, sling. Salves and two chemical pods. Journey-pan only — he forgot the protein, and said so.

**Noli** — held by projection against her terror of the barrier scent.

## IV. THE MISJUDGMENT LEDGER — what Act 1 must plant

For each adult: what the teenagers believe, what is true, and where the reader gets evidence they don't. **The reader collects; the characters do not.** This is the work the act currently isn't doing.

| adult | the teenagers see | what is true | where the reader can be given it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Joram & Riel** | cowards, paralysed with everyone else; protective to the point of insult | they already buried a daughter. Their caution is knowledge, not fear, and they of all people know what the First Walk cost | 3.1 — they stand apart, silent, *because they know the number*. 4.1 — the smell of their gear, the rationed food, the sister nobody names |
| **Marek** (Benal's father) | dismissive, pitying, ashamed of his son | he is the only adult publicly demanding action, and he is right. His pity is fear for a fragile child, not contempt | 2.2 against 3.1 — the man who says "do something real" at home is the man shouting for Genesis in the chamber |
| **Omya** | gone; a failure to be grieved | she is still trying, in public, at the cost of her dignity, because three Masters are required and she is the only one left | 1.1 — she submits to being tested in front of witnesses |
| **Hesh** | an obstacle | **decent, wise, and still to be opposed.** His objection is arithmetic, not cowardice: 106 dead, with better equipment than the children will have | 3.1 — his case is correct and goes unanswered |
| **Sila** | a zealot | **right about the danger, and her answer is still unforgivable.** She does not soften and is not redeemed | 3.1 — "better to die with a clean soul," said in a week when children are dying |

**The nuance has to cut three ways, or it collapses into "defer to your elders."**

Four adults deserve better than they got — Joram, Riel, Marek, Omya.

**Hesh is decent, correct on the facts, and must still be opposed.** This is the only genuinely adult lesson in the book and the plan was missing it. Nuance is not "they had reasons, so it's fine." It is "they had good reasons and I have to stop them anyway."

**And Sila is right about the danger, and her answer is still unforgivable.** The trace risks are real and unsolvable — see `prompts/milieu-brief.md` §6. She is not paranoid and she is not stupid; she has correct premises and draws a monstrous conclusion from them, in a week when children are dying for want of a vector. Without her the epiphany is cheap absolution and a YA reader will feel taught at.

**So it is a ladder, not a binary.** Misjudged. Right and opposed. Right and unforgivable.

Hesh is also **book 2's antagonist**, and he gets there by changing rather than by staying the same. Having accepted the revelation, he leads the suppression *for good reasons* — a society that learns it has twenty years and no way out will tear itself apart, and he is the man holding the casualty figures for what panic costs. So book 2 is the trio watching a decent man build a new Noble Lie out of the same materials the founders used, and having to decide whether he's wrong. Act 1 must therefore leave Hesh **intact and unrefuted**: his argument is never beaten, only overtaken by events.

Sila is a poor antagonist for the same reason she is a good non-changer — she is fully known by the end of her first scene, and a zealot who cannot be argued with is a wall rather than an opponent.

**Explicit budget for the whole theme: one line.** Teva's "they're not liars, they're believers" at beat 14. Everything else stays structural. If any character says they have learned that adults are complicated, the book dies.

The plant already on the page and not yet paid: `content/superseded/03.1.md:70` — Keo looks at his father, "his hero, the man who had faced down a saropo herd," and finds only fear. The reader should later learn what that fear was actually about.

## V. WHAT ACT 1 IS MISSING

Named here so the redraft doesn't reproduce it.

1. **Nobody is endearing.** Across the drafted prose there are two laughs, one grin and two smiles, and almost all of them sit in the scene immediately before Noli dies. Not one line of dialogue is funny. All three characters are built purely from wounds. Beat 2 is where the charm belongs, and Noli is where most of it should come from — she has to be worth losing.
2. **No inside jokes.** The trio should have shorthand by the time they leave. The strongest kind is a joke made in Act 1 that stops being funny in Act 3.
3. **Benal's gallows humour hasn't started.** He carries the book's comic register — deadpan under terror, the only control available to someone helpless — and it should be audible before the perimeter, not invented in beat 8.
4. **The romantic triangle is a label, not a texture.** Keo → Teva → Ben exists in the outline and nowhere in the prose. Act 1 is where the reader learns to want something.
5. **The epigraph channel is barely open.** One archival fragment exists (`content/superseded/01.1.md:1-5`) and it's good. The suite needs designing as a unit — see `.ignored/process-design-v2.md` §3.5 — and Act 1 is where the 21st century has to acquire a face.
