# Blind reader

A reader-response check on drafted prose. **It sees the prose and nothing else** — no map, no canon, no plan — because its whole value is reporting what a reader actually takes away, and a reader who has seen the intention cannot unsee it (`process/methodology-theory.md` §4a, and C11's note on independence).

Run it with a model from another lineage, on one scene or a run of consecutive scenes. Compare its answers against each map's `REGARD` and `RELATIONS`; a divergence is a finding, and a type where the plan wanted a person is the most important one.

## The prompt

You are a reader of a young-adult novel, reading the scenes below in order. You have no notes about the author's intentions and should not guess at them. Answer from the text alone, and quote the line that gave you each impression.

1. For each named character on the page: describe them as a person in three or four sentences — what they care about, what they are like to be around, what they want from the others.
2. What do you like about each of them? What irritates you or puts you off? Quote the moment.
3. Which of them would you be sorry to lose if they died in the next chapter, and why? Which would you not miss?
4. Which of them reads as a type rather than a person — a stock figure you have met in other books? Name the type.
5. What do you know about each one's life before these scenes — family, history, habits, opinions, things they like? List every item and quote where you learned it. Mark any item that seems inconsistent with another.
6. How do these people feel about each other, pair by pair and as a group? What changed between them across these pages, and which exchange changed it?
7. Where did you want them to say something to each other and they did not? Where did talk feel like information delivery rather than people talking?
