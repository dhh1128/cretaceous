# Pacing and the four stake ladders

Planning layer. Sets scene count, scene sizing, and the four stakes curves — which are one problem, because the ladders are what tell you where scenes are needed.

---

## 1. Where we actually are

`scene-list-v4.md` calls itself a 40-scene list. It contains **34**, and the distribution is the whole diagnosis:

| beats | scenes |
|---|---|
| 1–9 | **28** |
| **10–15** | **6 — one apiece** |

Six consecutive beats get one scene each, and they are: the B-story climax where the chosen family is forged, the discovery of the protocols, the revelation that voids six generations, the action climax, the confrontation, and the final image. **The entire emotional and structural payload of the novel is running at one scene per beat.**

At the canon's measured rate of ~1,058 words per scene, the current list yields about 36,000 words. The target is 70–100k.

## 2. Two holes that matter more than the arithmetic

**Keo's parents never speak to him on the page.** Scene 2.1 is Keo with Noli. In 3.1 he sees Joram across a crowded chamber. In 4.1 he robs them while they sleep, and Riel's only line is a slurred *"Keo?"* in the dark. **The entire parent-nuance arc — the novel's stated purpose — rests on two adults who never have a conversation with their son.** There is nothing to plant the misjudgment ledger with because the relationship has never been dramatized.

**Omya never speaks to Teva.** In 1.1 the grandmother is already failing, and recognition flickers and dies. Teva's drive is grief for a woman disappearing, and the reader never meets the woman. Beat 10's bridge speech — *"I left her while she's disappearing"* — has nothing behind it.

Both are Act 1 problems and both need a scene. Everything else here is adjustment; these are missing organs.

## 2b. Chapters — an unmade decision

**Cretaceous has no chapter concept at all.** The plan is 60 scenes and nothing above them. That is a real gap, and the two novels solve it in opposite ways:

- **viking** uses `§` as an in-chapter scene break — 39 of them across 55 chapters. Chapters hold multiple scenes and the break carries the transition, so **the first clause after a `§` does the time-skip inside it** rather than announcing it. No "meanwhile," no "later that day" standing alone.
- **cordimancy** uses **zero** internal breaks. Every chapter is one unbroken scene, headed by an italic label of the form `*subject ~ POV*` — *the antechild ~ Malena, a decade later*, *boy on naming day ~ Toril*. The subject phrase is always a concrete object or event, never a theme.

**The cordimancy label is worth serious consideration here**, because it solves two Cretaceous-specific problems at once. This is a **rotating three-POV book**, so the reader needs to know whose head they are in before the first sentence rather than four lines down. And Act 2 is an **eleven-day journey with unscened days** — Day 6, Day 9, Day 10 — where a label absorbing "*two days on*" costs nothing and a bridging paragraph costs a page.

The cost is that it is a visible formal device, and it is not a YA-thriller convention.

**Open, and it should be decided before Act 2 is rescened**, because it changes what a scene has to do at its opening.

## 3. Scene sizing doctrine

Page-turning is not a function of short scenes. It comes from three things, and the third is where richness lives.

**Every scene ends on a hook** — a question, a turn, or a threat. No scene closes on summary or on a settled feeling. This is the single largest lever and it costs nothing.

**Length runs inversely to tension.** Short scenes at crisis points, because the white space between them is where the reader's dread does its work; longer scenes for consolidation and world, where the reader wants to stay. Never two consecutive scenes of the same shape.

**Richness is a function of scene count, not scene length.** This is the part that resolves the "rich world but never slow" tension. A world feels deep when each scene shows one new true thing — a different hour, weather, biome, ritual, sensory allocation — not when one scene shows twenty. More scenes means more distinct places to *be*. Long scenes make a world feel thorough; many scenes make it feel large.

Three sizes, mixed deliberately:

| size | words | use |
|---|---|---|
| **short** | 400–900 | action beats, hard cuts, a single image, a scene that exists to land one blow |
| **medium** | 1,000–1,600 | the workhorse — what the canon naturally writes |
| **long** | 1,800–2,500 | consolidation, revelation, the emotional set-pieces |

## 5. The four ladders

Emotional, Physical, Social, Species. Rough 0–10 by beat.

| beat | E | P | S | X | what moves |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | 1 | 2 | 3 | rage ignites; the community fails in public |
| 2 | 3 | 1 | 3 | 3 | *rest beat — the deposit* |
| 3 | 5 | 2 | 5 | 4 | paralysis made official |
| 4 | 6 | 4 | **8** | 4 | the theft destroys two families' standing |
| 5 | 5 | 7 | 8 | 4 | first blood |
| 6 | 6 | 7 | 6 | 5 | flaws surface under load |
| 7 | **9** | 8 | 6 | 5 | Noli |
| 8 | 7 | 9 | 6 | 5 | injury, suit failure |
| 9 | 8 | **10** | 7 | 6 | drowning, the mosasaur, the fieldpack lost |
| 10 | **10** | 5 | 7 | 6 | the confessions; chosen family forged |
| 11 | 7 | 4 | 7 | 8 | the protocols are real |
| 12 | 8 | 4 | 8 | **10** | fact G — the mission was never possible |
| 13 | 8 | **10** | 9 | 8 | the chase |
| 14 | 9 | 5 | **10** | 9 | the confrontation |
| 15 | **10** | 3 | 10 | 9 | the parents |

**Act 3 gives each beat one ladder's summit, in sequence: species at 12, physical at 13, social at 14, emotional at 15.** That is the answer to "ratchet and converge," and it is also the justification for sixteen scenes — each beat has a distinct job rather than sharing one.

### The diagnosis

**Two ladders are flat through the entire middle of the book.**

**Social** goes dormant at beat 5 and does not move again until 14, because the trio leaves everyone who could raise it. Act 2 runs on physical stakes almost alone, which is why a ten-day walk risks reading as walking with monsters attached.

**Species** barely moves anywhere — 3,3,4,4,4,5,5,5,6,6 — and it is the ladder Daniel most wants to feel. It's abstract, and abstractions don't climb.

### The fixes

**Social, in Act 2: the ladder is intra-trio standing.** Who leads, who is trusted, who is carried, who is a liability. It's already in the B-story notes as "skills clash" and it just needs to be tracked as a stake rather than a theme. Plus two external pressures they can feel without being present for: **compounding guilt** — what they have done to their families is getting worse while they cannot see it — and the fact that they do not know whether their parents are confined, punished, or dead.

**Species, in Act 2: the ladder lives in the epigraph channel.** This is the fix that makes everything else work. **Marisol is dying across the fragments.** Cecilia's letters escalate — her sister is well, then symptomatic, then not answering, then someone else writes. The species stakes climb in one channel while the physical stakes climb in the other, and they braid. The reader feels the future dying on a schedule, in a voice they have come to love, while three children walk through a swamp.

That probably means **more fragments than the seven in the knowledge ledger — call it nine to eleven** — because a ladder needs rungs. It also means the fragments are no longer only an irony instrument; they are the only place the novel's largest stake can actually move.

### The rule that governs scene count

**Every scene moves at least one ladder.** A scene that moves none is cut. **A scene that moves three is probably two scenes** — which is exactly what has gone wrong at beats 12 and 14, and it is the principled reason to split them rather than a feeling about length.

And **the ladders must not all move together.** If all four climb in every scene the book is monotonous and exhausting; beat 2 is deliberately a rest on three of four, which is what makes beat 4 land.
