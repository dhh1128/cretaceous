# CRETACEOUS — ACT 3 CONTEXT (Genesis & The Return)

Beats 11–15, scenes 11.1 through 15.1. Days 12–13.

Completes the set with `act-1-context.md` and `act-2-context.md`. Read alongside `prompts/knowledge-ledger.md`, which governs everything in this act.

---

## 0. THE STRUCTURAL PROBLEM TO SOLVE FIRST

**Act 3 is five scenes.** Act 1 is ten, Act 2 is nineteen. So the climax, the revelation, the return journey, the confrontation and the final image together get 12.5% of the scene count for what should carry something closer to a quarter of the book — and at the canon's measured rate of ~1,058 words per scene, all of Act 3 lands around 5,300 words.

That will not hold what it has to hold. Beat 13 is an entire action climax in one scene. Beat 14 is the confrontation, the resolution of the misjudgment ledger for four adults, three completed arcs, and the failure of fact B, in one scene.

**Act 3 needs to roughly double, to eight or nine scenes.** The natural splits: 12.1 into discovery and comprehension; 13.1 into finding the wig, the chase, and the arrival; 14.1 into the handover of the protocols and the confrontation, which are two different scenes doing two different jobs and currently share one.

---

## I. WHAT THIS ACT HAS TO ACCOMPLISH

**The trio catches up to the reader.** This is the governing principle and it changes how the act should feel. Under the knowledge allocation the reader has been ahead on seven counts since Act 2. So beats 11–14 are not instruction — they are **release**. The pleasure is watching them arrive somewhere the reader has been waiting, which is a different and better sensation than being informed.

Only one thing in this act is genuinely new to the reader: **fact G**, that only the original colonists could ever have returned. That is why it is the climax.

**Three surprises of the budgeted four land here** — the powered section at 11.1, fact G at 12.1, and the Lost Colonists opening at the end. Nothing else in the act may be structured as a reveal.

## II. BEAT BY BEAT

### BEAT 11 — Break Into Three (11.1, Benal)

Ben's senses find it: a hum, a smell, a sealed hatch. Inside, a dry powered section, and the complete synthesis protocols on metal tablets.

*Surprise 2, relief-shaped, landing directly on "all is lost."* The cheapest and most reliable emotional swing in the book — do not undercut it with foreboding. Let them win for one scene.

*And the first sight of the cache.* Records the colony has only in degraded partial copies, plus fragments nobody has ever had. This is where the epigraph channel turns diegetic and the reader understands what they have been reading since page one.

### BEAT 12 — The Revelation (12.1, Benal)

Currently one scene stacking three revelations. Under the new allocation it is mostly **comprehension, not discovery**, and it should split.

- **The logs of the perfect arrival.** The mission worked. Everything after was drift.
- **Benal's epiphany.** Triggered by an offhand frustrated line from Keo or Teva — *"It's like they just left. Why couldn't they just go back?"* He connects the equation, the flash from 8.4, and the logs. **Fact G: there is no return anchor. Only the original colonists could ever have gone back. Everyone born here is stranded by birth.** This is the largest surprise in the novel and it voids six generations of waiting.
- **The second jump**, and the symbol from Keo's flash — the signal the heretics received.
- **The founders' error was drift, not deceit.** The colony's copies are missing exactly the passages that would have corrected it. The reader, who has been led to convict the founders, is corrected here.

**And the beat nobody planned: they cannot carry it all.** Three exhausted teenagers choose which records come home, under time pressure, and the choice will shape their society permanently. Put it here or at 13.1. It is the best decision-under-pressure available in the act and it costs nothing to add.

### BEAT 13 — The Heretic's Ride (13.1, Keo)

The old-tech wig. Their first act of open heresy. Full spec: `kb/worldbuilding/the-wig.md`.

**It is a wing, not a rotor.** It cannot hover — lift comes from forward speed, so once launched they are committed and cannot stop. It clears obstacles by trading speed for altitude and diving to recover, which means **altitude and speed are the same currency** and every tree costs them.

**The cyclone is what makes it possible.** Eighty kilometers of flooded coastal plain is exactly the surface a ground-effect craft wants, and it did not exist a week ago. **The storm that closed the walking route opened the flying one** — and woodland still stands out of the flood, so the route home is a problem of finding the line with the fewest things to jump. **Teva gets her navigation back here**, in a form that matters more than it ever did on foot, after losing it offshore at 9.2.

**The chase is a resource crisis, not a stunt.** The flybeak forces them to climb; climbing bleeds speed; bleeding speed means sinking; below is water with things in it. Every evasion makes the next one harder. **Keo's redemption is arithmetic under pressure rather than courage** — he is not braver than he was at the midpoint, he is *thinking*, with a machine, about a problem mental projection cannot touch. That is what completes hubris → humility → competence.

**Guard:** the walk out took nine days and the return takes hours. That only works if the return is a *different* ordeal rather than a reward — faster, louder, wholly out of their control, and frightening precisely because they cannot stop it.

**The landing is the last beat of Keo's arc.** They clear the border-tangle by almost nothing — fronds on the hull — and he puts the wig down **on the river channel**, not on the easy floodwater everywhere else, because the channel is the only water still there when the flood recedes. The AI tells him that; he has to decide whether to trust a machine his people call an abomination, telling him to land in the water his people call death. He is doing arithmetic about three months from now while something is chasing him, which is the opposite of the boy who refused a suit to prove a point.

**It survives, and that is deliberate.** A wrecked wig is a story that ends. An intact solar-recharging one is the object book 2 argues about — and it must live on water, in the open, where it cannot be hidden or moved inland. **The only place they can keep the most valuable thing they own is the one place this culture has spent 206 years telling its children never to go.** Never state this.

*This should be three or four scenes.* Finding and understanding the machine; launching it; the chase; the arrival and the landing.

### BEAT 14 — The Confrontation (14.1, Keo)

Two jobs currently sharing one scene, and they should not. **The handover** — they give the Enclave the protocols and save the kiva — is triumph. **The confrontation** — walking into the Council and breaking the story their society tells about itself — is something else entirely. Split them.

**The misjudgment ledger resolves here.** See `act-1-context.md` §IV. Keo understands his parents; Benal understands Marek; Teva sees the Council's faces and recognizes the faces from her flash at 10.1. **Sila does not change.** Hesh is not refuted — only overtaken.

**Teva's line is the entire explicit budget for the theme:** *they're not liars, they're believers.* Nothing else in the novel states it.

**And fact B fails to land.** Benal says the upgrades are the cure. He cannot demonstrate it — the timeline came with proof, this comes with inference. The room has no bandwidth for what the upgrades are *for* in the hour it learns it has twenty years. He says it a second time, and **Sila takes it up enthusiastically as confirmation of everything she has always believed**, having understood nothing. He is not disbelieved; he is absorbed. Full mechanism and rationale in `knowledge-ledger.md`.

*Guard:* the timeline revelation must land hard, or the scene reads as anticlimax. Beat 14 is a success with one splinter in it, not a failure.

### BEAT 15 — Final Image (15.1, Keo)

The stunned Council. Keo's parents under guard, meeting his eyes with vindicated pride. Marek looking at his son with shocked respect. The three standing together, no longer children.

**The coda: a fragment they left behind.** The correction of the false solution, read by the reader over their shoulders as they picked up something else and went. The characters walk away from the answer. The reader holds it. And the rest of the cache is still out there, offshore and unstable, and now everyone knows — which is the contested ground book 2 opens on.

**Surprise 4 lands here and stays dark: the Lost Colonists exist.** The reader must end the book knowing less than they want to.

---

## III. WHAT ACT 3 MUST NOT DO

- **Explain.** The reader has been ahead since Act 1. Anything delivered as new information that the ledger says is ironic will read as the book repeating itself.
- **Resolve the Lost Colonists.** Not a hint of what became of them.
- **Redeem Sila**, or refute Hesh.
- **Let anyone articulate the theme** beyond Teva's one line.
- **Give the Enclave fact B.** See the ledger.
- **Land the humour.** Keo's bravado died at the midpoint and stays dead; Benal's gallows register is the only comic voice left, and Act 3 gets almost none of it until the final image, where one small callback does more than a paragraph.
