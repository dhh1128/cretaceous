# Style canon

**Verbatim passages from Daniel Hardman's two hand-written novels.** This ships with every drafting call. It is the primary instrument; `writing-style.md` is the annotation on it.

Sources: `~/code/me/viking/viking.md` (91,955 words) and `~/code/me/cordimancy/cordimancy.md` (124,545 words). **Viking is closer to Cretaceous's target voice** — cordimancy is more meditative, more theologically dense, more adult — so the weighting here is about two to one, and cordimancy passages appear where they do something viking doesn't.

Quotes spot-checked against source, 2026-09-09. Line numbers point at the passage's first line.

**Do not use `content/superseded/` for voice.** Those scenes are edited AI drafts and measure at mean 7.7 / median 6 against the numbers below.

## 0. The fingerprint

| | viking | cordimancy |

| mean sentence | 11.2 | 11.0 |
| median | 9 | 9 |

**Short sentences cluster at paragraph *ends* as beat-closers, and are frequently paragraphs of their own.** A model told to write punchy prose distributes them evenly inside paragraphs. That is the single most common way to sound almost right and be wrong.

## 1. Openings — in medias res

**[sensation arrives before orientation]** `viking.md:22`
> Gravity reached out with a million sticky fingers and jerked Rafa in a different direction than he had expected. He felt his stomach lurch and his harness stretch taut around hips and thighs. The entire module shuddered as wind shrieked across its hull.

The first noun is a force, not a person. Three body sensations before the reader learns there is a ship, a planet or a crew.

**[opens mid-collapse, on the wrong person]** `viking.md:2948`
> Despite Chen's repeated stumbles, it was Abbott who gave out first. One minute he was reeling drunkenly along, a crust of spittle around his lips; the next he had crumpled to the grasses without a word.

"Despite" presumes a run already in progress and a reader already tracking who is likeliest to fall.

**[waking, with the questions paced on the page]** `viking.md:5978`
> Rafa opened his eyes feeling disoriented. It was black in the tent, and breezes rolling off the surf twitched the styrocil near his cheek.
>
> How had he known this was a tent?
>
> Where was he?
>
> What woke him up? He was uneasy for some reason.

Each question is its own paragraph. Note the second-order question — *how had he known this was a tent* — which makes the reader distrust the narration.

**[the word "again" does the exposition]** `cordimancy.md:38`
> Toril stepped forward again, letting river sweep over his head as he sank.

One adverb places the reader some unknown distance into a ritual. The missing article in "letting river sweep" is deliberate and recurs — **do not normalize it.**

**[chapter opens on a bare line of dialogue that demands a response]** `cordimancy.md:3115`
> "Blink at me if you're still alive," Kinora hissed.

Situation, relationship, danger level and the speaker's age, in nine words. It works because it is an *instruction*, so the scene has somewhere to go.

## 2. Endings — a turn, an image, a threat, a question. Never a summary.

**[countdown into absence]** `viking.md:1874`
> And then nothing.
>
> No sight, no sound, no smell.
>
> No breathing.
>
> No heartbeat.
>
> No Rafa.

Paragraphs shorten to two words; the negations run from external senses inward to the man.

**[three beats of reaction before the fact that caused them]** `viking.md:5498`
> He stopped crawling.
>
> He stopped breathing.
>
> Behind, a heavy wave crashed, raining salty mist on his raw neck and ears. But he took no notice.
>
> The hair along the nape of his neck bristled.
>
> Human hands had not embossed these words.

The fact is stated as a flat negative — what the words *aren't* — rather than "aliens."

**[one clause closes a two-page fall]** `viking.md:4962`
> Then the forest swallowed him.

Five words, active verb, no adjective. The abstraction is earned by a preceding page of nothing but concrete detail.

**[ends on the implement, not the outcome]** `viking.md:220`
> He jerked a thumb at Chen, who knelt with her medikit. Fazio wasn't breathing right. Rafa turned away as Chen swabbed his throat and selected a scalpel.

Whether the tracheotomy works is never told, here or later. The reader is left holding the scalpel.

**[ends on paralysis in both directions]** `cordimancy.md:2248`
> She stood in the dark until the sound behind her stopped. Then she stood a while longer, because she could not make herself move in either direction.

The emotional beat is made physical — a countable ten paces — and the final clause refuses to choose.

## 3. Transitions and time skips

**[the break carries the jump; the first clause absorbs it]** `viking.md:3036`
> §
>
> They huddled around a small fire, their shoulders rounded to the velvet night, their faces animated by leaping hues of gold and scarlet.

Afternoon to full dark in one break, established by firelight and stars. **There is no "meanwhile" and no standalone "later that day."** Other instances: *Three hours later, Satler stifled a yawn…* · *By the time they lifted off, full night had fallen.* · *Back in the commons, the floor was littered with…*

**[the chapter label eats the transition]** `cordimancy.md:197`
> *the antechild ~ Malena, a decade later*

Every cordimancy chapter carries `*subject ~ POV*`, and the subject phrase is always a concrete object or event, never a theme. **Under consideration for Cretaceous** — see `pacing-and-stakes.md` §2b.

## 4. Action — the rhythm rule that matters most

**[long clause carries the run; short lines are isolated and count distance]** `viking.md:1860`
> Behind, an ominous thunder began to swell. Rafa cast away the rifle and lengthened his stride. The air whistled through his clenched teeth, and Julie could feel the see-sawing tension across shoulder and pectoral muscles as his arms pumped savagely and his heart galloped in unison.
>
> He stumbled in a small depression.
>
> "Come on," Julie groaned. "Come on, Rafa."
>
> The rock was still fifty meters away.
>
> Forty.
>
> [...]
>
> Twenty meters.

**This is the most important passage in the file.** The running itself lives inside a 46-word compound sentence. The short sentences are never stacked — each is its own paragraph doing one job. **The staccato comes from paragraph breaks, not from clipped syntax.**

**[cause before effect, so the move is a decision]** `viking.md:2962`
> Half a dozen crabbies pounded into his chest and torso in unison, their jutting legs stiffening for the impact like daggers. Individually the animals were not especially heavy, but even before they hit he knew his tired legs wouldn't hold. So he somersaulted backward, minimizing the effect of the blows and letting the creatures' momentum flip his shoulders onto and off the turf again. As he rotated he stabbed with the knife, the point skittering across chitinous shell until it found joints in the armor that gave reluctantly like under-ripe pumpkin.

The simile lands on **texture** — resistance under a blade — not on how it looked.

**[the fight is two sentences; the paragraph after is bookkeeping]** `viking.md:6040`
> Satisfied, his arm flashed up, then plunged… and slammed into sand.
>
> A cast smashed Heward's nose and conjured stars, even as Orosco rolled under the side of the tent.
>
> Heward vented an oath. He could feel blood streaming down his face from cheekbone abrasions and a crushed nasal bridge. A tooth had mangled his lower lip. But he had no time for inventory; Orosco was out of the tent.

Nineteen words of violence, then damage inventory and tactical consequence in calm full sentences.

**[disorientation by removing the reference frame]** `cordimancy.md:2599`
> All sense of orientation disappeared. She was tumbling out of control. Which way was up?
>
> A foot kicked her shoulder. A hand clawed at her braid.

Once the POV loses orientation, sentences lose subjects with agency and report discrete inputs. The rescuer is felt as an assailant for four sentences before being identified.

## 5. The body under duress

**[anaphora, then a countercurrent]** `viking.md:1526`
> Every muscle ached. Every nerve had been strained beyond tolerance. Every joint, every bone felt hammered and spent. He was seeing double and his eyes were red. [...] But despite the weariness he felt a glimmer of something approaching satisfaction.

Three rising "Every" sentences, two concrete symptoms, then a turn. **Exhaustion is never the last note of a duress paragraph.**

**[hours compressed to a loop with a measured result]** `viking.md:5310`
> He crawled until his forearms trembled and sweat dripped in his eyes and the heat of the day had stoked the jungle to an oven and every cell cried out for water. He drank with unsteady hands and studied his nav and despaired to find a scant two hundred meters of linear progress.
>
> Then when he was strong enough, he raised a slimy fist and started all over again.

The unbroken *and…and…and* refuses to let the reader rest. Hours of suffering, then **one number** for the reward.

**[a body detail proved by what it can no longer do]** `cordimancy.md:118`
> Fresh moisture would have welled from his eyes, if his tear ducts had not been frozen shut. A groan died in his chest.

## 6. Dread

**[dread as a logic problem with a missing term]** `viking.md:2348`
> A feast this big would attract a lot more than vultures back on earth. Where were the jackals, the hyenas, and the lions of this ecosystem? Not here.
>
> Not yet, anyway.
>
> Perhaps they were nocturnal.
>
> Rafa shuddered.

**No adjective of fear anywhere.** The dread is manufactured by an *absence* the character reasons toward.

**[long anticipation; the event refuses to be shown]** `viking.md:3796`
> The recoil was so fast that it could not be seen. One moment an inky tendril was touching flame; the next, leaves and branches were fluttering down, torn by the whip crack of retraction.

He does not narrate an instantaneous thing instant by instant. Before-state and after-state only.

## 7. Sensory rationing

**[a whole day of alien forest gets three sentences]** `viking.md:4228`
> As they walked, the scent of decaying leaves, wet chlorophyll and fungus filled the air. The loudest sounds came from the crunch of boots on the forest floor and the whip of disturbed branches and leaves.

One smell, one sound, one sight — and **the loudest thing in the scene is the party's own boots.**

**[first contact with a biosphere is spent on a nose]** `viking.md:1012`
> He breathed in the scents of the atmosphere. Ozone, tinged with hints of greenery and flowers. Smelled like a park or a garden after a storm on Earth. Smelled alive.

Temperature first, sound second, sight third, then a whole paragraph for smell.

**[interoception, not scenery]** `cordimancy.md:74`
> Toril heard the eddy downstream of his ribs. He felt rays strike his shoulders through the gloom, knew exactly which muscles were lit and which were bathed in shade, understood the gradations of clamminess permeating flesh and bone. [...] He sensed thousands of hairs along his calves and forearms twitch in buffeting water.

Every clause is a boundary between body and world. Sun as **differential warmth across muscle groups**, not as light.

## 8. Weather as consequence

**[the storm exists only as a resource to be routed]** `viking.md:5122`
> Then saner reasoning prevailed. Rain made its way to the forest floor eventually. He simply had to find its path. He pulled out his flashlight and directed it up at the jade thatch. The beam hit pearls falling silently in every direction.

The one lyrical image is earned because it is also the tactical information he needs.

**[temperature converted into differential survival per body type]** `cordimancy.md:2589`
> Paka seemed to cope with the water and the temperature well enough, but Shivi was a problem; the older woman was too petite to retain body warmth, and her comfort with water was obviously low.

Exactly the calculus a long-journey survival novel runs on.

## 9. Dialogue — transactional

**[flat inventory; emotion carried between the lines, not inside them]** `viking.md:106`
> "We lost three. Cooper had severe internal bleeding. Begay broke his neck. Ahmad had enough trauma to the cranium to kill her three times over. [...] He'll make it, though."
>
> The tightness around her eyes and cheeks suggested otherwise. But what could you say when the man was listening in his bunk a couple steps away?
>
> "Medical supplies?"

Proper names, injuries, no adjectives. Nobody thanks anybody or acknowledges the dead. The follow-up question is two words.

**[the good idea arrives as a question, is resisted, and the credit is withheld]** `viking.md:552`
> "Wait a minute." Rafa was also struggling for breath. He swallowed. "Had a thought. Can't we activate some of the machinery in the hold by remote?"
>
> "Yeah. So what?" Heward looked annoyed.
>
> "Don't we have a bulldozer or something that could take this door out for us?"
>
> Understanding flooded across Heward's face.

**Nobody explains their own cleverness.** The recognition happens on the other person's face, and twelve lines later: *"Nobody looked at Rafa."*

**[deduction under pressure; one-word interrogatives because nobody has breath]** `viking.md:3842`
> "I was wrong. This is no display of temper."
>
> [...]
>
> "Why?" Chen wheezed.
>
> "Process of elimination. It knows we're in here somewhere, but it didn't like what happened when it reached in blind. So it's weeding itself a clearing until it either finds us or flushes us out."

The correction comes *before* the reasoning. "Chen wheezed" does attribution and physiology in one verb.

**[five expertises in a circle; every proposal killed by whoever owns the domain]** `cordimancy.md:2457`
> "When they're asleep, we could sneak…" Toril began.
>
> Oji was already shaking his head. "You might get past a human sentry, but there's no way you'll slip past ahu. I might do it, but not humans. Especially not humans on horses."
>
> "We could go around the other side of the mountain," Paka offered.
>
> "Way too far," said Oji. "We couldn't get back to the river by morning."

Each objection is specific enough to be checkable — smell, distance, moonrise, tree density. **The group leaves with a plan they know is bad.** This is the model for the Day 3 crossing.

## 10. Dialogue — subtext and indirection

**[a murder ordered entirely in logistics]** `viking.md:4414`
> "How many seats up front in the skimmer?"
>
> "Eight."
>
> "The small one?"
>
> "Oh. It's got three. But it's been running rough."
>
> "Hope it gets those poor vikings back here in one piece."

Neither man ever says the plan. It is assembled from a reminiscence, a seating chart and a good wish.

**[a proposition and a refusal, neither one stated]** `viking.md:3472`
> "A man shouldn't have to die lonely." She looked him in the eye, her head inclined with meaning.
>
> Rafa's face was a study in motionless stone and shadow.
>
> When he spoke again his voice was thin. "This one will." And he pulled her hand away.

Both phrase it as a general observation about men. **The refusal reuses her own sentence frame**, which is why it lands as tender rather than cruel.

**[answering a question with a question about something not yet explained]** `viking.md:4350`
> "Besides, we've got to get Chen back."
>
> "Chen?"
>
> Rafa stopped walking, the crows feet near his eyes deepening. "You've never seen video of a joaker dying, have you?"

The chapter ends there. The answer is withheld from the listener and the reader at once — **and nobody explains what a joaker is.**

## 11. Attribution

**[fourteen untagged lines, one re-anchoring beat at the midpoint]** `viking.md:2432`
> "Maybe something was damaged. They were parked awfully close to the herd when it all started."
>
> "Maybe."
>
> "Or maybe the skimmer doesn't carry any homing equipment and they went back to get it."
>
> "That could be." It was the first explanation that gave Rafa any hope.
>
> [...]
>
> "Including us."
>
> "Exactly."

He *deliberately does not differentiate* here — three exhausted people speculating in the dark are interchangeable on purpose. And the run closes on a joke nobody laughs at.

**[diction alone separates the speakers]** `viking.md:132`
> "You're lookin' at it," said the kid. "Dead center." He nursed the lollipop.
>
> "There's nothing there but a huge pile of mud and rocks."
>
> "Keep lookin'."

The tell is **stance**, not accent — the kid enjoys knowing something his commander doesn't.

**[physiology costs a character the floor]** `viking.md:144`
> "We *are* buried alive!" Whemper's shout was nasal and whining. "We'll never dig out of this!" He opened his mouth to say more, but subsided in a fit of mucus-heavy coughs.
>
> "We ain't buried," said the kid.

He is cut off mid-point, someone else takes the floor, and he has to fight back in. The body business is placed **after** the line, so it reads as the price of having spoken.

## 12. Speech that is not spoken aloud

**[italics, no quotes, no tags — the typography does it]** `viking.md:4850`
> *Erisa Explorer, this is Dr. Edvardsen. The emergency beacon tells us you're there and alive, but it isn't set up to relay your implants. So I'm afraid this will be a one-sided conversation.*

Paragraph breaks become the beats where listeners would have interrupted. Reaction deferred to prose afterward.

**[two channels alternating — the model for Keo and the AI]** `viking.md:6408`
> *You may not know this, but I've been following you for a while*, Geire began.
>
> "How's that?"
>
> [...]
>
> "So I was a spy."
>
> *You were.*
>
> "A mole."
>
> Geire hesitated. *If you want to call it that. But all in a good cause.*

The mediated voice gets long fluent paragraphs and flat confirmations; the aloud voice gets short statements that aren't even questions and get answered anyway.

**[alien-ness lives in substituted nouns inside plain syntax]** `viking.md:5708`
> *1291, we have heard you rebroadcasting its speech.*
>
> *A few times. That is true.*
>
> *Why did you do that?*
>
> *I thought it might be trying to call the siren in The Cold.*

Prim, almost formal English. The strangeness is entirely in what gets *named*. Emotion for faceless beings is rendered as body state — *a yellow blush of amusement from 3, cherry surprise on 2 and 5*.

## 13. Stillness — hands, not talk

**[a hand-task carries the scene]** `viking.md:3048`
> Chen observed his whittling without expression. "Think it'll do any good?" she asked.
>
> "What?"
>
> "Your spear."
>
> Rafa looked at her across the flames.
>
> "Can't hurt."
>
> Abbott stopped humming *Amazing Grace* and shook his head. "Easier to let 'em get us. Put us out of our misery."
>
> Rafa kept whittling.

The whittling is pain endured, a defense being built, and a reason not to look at anyone. **"Can't hurt" answers the spear and the despair at once.**

**[the group names the activity out loud]** `viking.md:3096`
> "Okay, I'll bite," said Chen. "Let's have a pity party. You go first."
>
> Abbott smiled thinly. "You want the short, dramatic version?"
>
> "No need to abridge. We're a captive audience."

Naming it licenses forty lines of backstory without the scene reading as a file dump. *"We're a captive audience"* is the joke **and** the premise. **The load-bearing move for an Act 2 of enforced stillness.**

**[mundanity as warmth]** `viking.md:2498`
> "I left you a drink there. Tastes terrible."
>
> There was gurgling, then Chen's voice sounding more alert. "Like a cross between a duck pond and dishwater. Those purification tablets are nasty."

Three people who nearly died yesterday spend their first waking minute on urination, aftertaste and a stiff back. Nobody reflects on the situation.

## 14. Humour

**[bits, not quips — comic business others can watch]** `cordimancy.md:1908`
> He pulled a chunk of sizzling meat off the spit, seemed to notice just how hot it was, and began tossing it back and forth, blowing frantically on his fingertips. [...] Paka offered his wife the prize. "First bite, sweetheart?" he said, with as much dignity as he could muster.
>
> Shivi drew a small knife from her belt. "Perhaps I'll just spit it again," she said, her voice matter-of-fact.

**The deadpan straight man tops the comedian.** The funny one does not win his own exchange.

**[the laugh has something underneath it]** `viking.md:1440`
> When Whemper's face blanched, Heward burst into laughter. "Don't be a fool! You think I'd re-enlist if it had really been that bad?"

The joke is a small act of torture with a punchline, and the reader laughs a beat before realizing what they laughed at.

**[comic bravado as refusal to acknowledge fear]** `viking.md:2482`
> Abbott yawned mightily and rubbed his eyes. "Let's go. I've got dibs on the presidential suite," he said. "And I'll kill anyone who wakes me up. I plan to sleep in tomorrow."

The "presidential suite" is a thorn thicket. A man announcing a future he does not expect to have.

**[the joke that dies, and the death is the point]** `viking.md:4180`
> Chen smiled back and held out a hand as she rose. "Come on, lazy bones, I intend to be home tonight if it kills me."
>
> Nobody laughed.

**A two-word paragraph measuring exactly how much reserve the group has left.** He never lets a run of banter end on a laugh line. **A model will never write this**, because it reads like a joke that failed — which is what it is.

## 15. Grief and restraint

**[grief as absurdity, not weeping]** `viking.md:2124`
> For a moment the sight of it, lying shriveled, all alone on the white porcelain, raised such visions of vapid mundanity that she had an urge to burst into laughter. *Rafa's dead. Have a sausage.*
>
> Instead, she added some hash browns and toast and eggs, then dribbled syrup over the whole plate.

The impulse is toward **laughter**, which is truer and unavailable to sentimentality. Then she loads a plate she will not eat.

**[the reply arrives after she is dead]** `viking.md:6252`
> "Pray for me, Rafa. I need a friend."
>
> Then her eyes went wide, and her hand dropped.
>
> "You have one," Rafa whispered.
>
> Abbott blinked, kicked at the sand, and turned away.

The reader supplies the loss. The last beat goes to the third party, in three physical verbs.

**[a confession answered with a gesture]** `cordimancy.md:2618`
> He looked at Malena, waited for a comment.
>
> She twisted water from her braid.

He explicitly waits for a response and the text gives him a gesture. **Refusing the consolation the scene has set up is what makes it land.**

**[two words instead of the scene everyone expects]** `viking.md:3138`
> After a moment, Rafa rose and limped into the darkness without a word [...] He returned dragging some branches with his good arm and began to methodically snap them underfoot to feed the flames.
>
> "I'm cold," he said, as he slumped back to his knees near Abbott.
>
> Chen cocked her head sideways and smiled a melancholy little smile at her feet.

A rejection, an explanation and an apology compressed into a two-word lie about the weather — and she hears every layer, signalled by a smile and no dialogue. **He comes back and sits down; the refusal is not an exit.**

**[reconciliation delivered as inventory]** `cordimancy.md:2266`
> The sheepskin was spread on a knee-deep bed of dry needles. When she pulled it back, the aroma of toasted spruce tickled her nose. Two melon-sized stones nestled in the piles of padding, still radiating heat from the fire. Atop one lay a pair of clean, dry footwraps.

The estranged husband is not on the page, is not mentioned again, and is not thanked. **The chapter ends on the footwraps.**

## 16. Charm

**[a small, expensive, unnecessary kindness]** `viking.md:3484`
> "Chen." His voice was softer now.
>
> Again she looked back.
>
> "I may die lonely, but none of my friends will."

He turns her down twice and the second refusal is a gift. The charm is entirely in calling her name a second time rather than letting her walk away humiliated.

**[an animal made lovable by petulance]** `viking.md:4110`
> Instead they proposed to eat it! The dunces! Hadn't they had enough fish and sunshine? Couldn't they think about something besides their bellies? Here was an oddity unlike any they'd ever seen before, and they charted a direct course to food.

A fifty-meter flying alien becomes a specific person in two sentences, **entirely through exasperation with her peers.** The text never calls her endearing. **For a midpoint animal death to hurt, the animal must have had opinions first.**

**[a gift with the trick openly confessed]** `cordimancy.md:1940`
> "For you," she said. "For Paka's green feet and your happy memories."
>
> [...]
>
> "They're actually sprigs of chickweed," Shivi said. "I just wove a little magic to make them look like daisies."

The gift is admitted to be fake in the same breath it is given, and is more moving for it. It costs nothing and advances nothing — it exists so the reader will want these people to survive.

## 17. Interiority is argument, not narration of feeling

**[self-contempt quoted, not described]** `viking.md:3248`
> Sure, he'd pitched in to help Fazio. But that had been out of self-righteous indignation more than any deep-felt sympathy. [...] *Out of my way. Leave me alone. Quit complaining. You don't know how good you've got it. Mind your own business. Shut up and listen to my sob story*. It was an unimaginative litany, and he was disgusted to catch himself reciting it.

Each concession — *Sure, he'd… And he'd…* — is immediately taken back. **The paragraph argues with itself instead of narrating a conclusion.**

**[questions in threes, then self-overruling in fragments]** `cordimancy.md:2418`
> Wasn't she capable of deciding what was best for herself? What gave him the right to meddle? Did she want a husband who ran rough-shod over her feelings like that?
>
> [...]
>
> She shook her head. No, that wasn't fair. Wasn't even accurate.

The engine behind cordimancy's 653 non-dialogue question marks. **The self-correction is what keeps a page of thinking from being static — it has a winner and a loser in it.**

## 18. Worldbuilding without exposition

**[coinage minted on-page, definition withheld]** `viking.md:772`
> "Pufferbellies?"
>
> *We don't come up with fancy Latin names until we can fill in the taxonomy with DNA analysis.*

The character asks and gets a reply about **naming policy**, not about the animal. The word attaches to a memory rather than a definition, and is then used forty more times with no gloss.

**[invented material in an adverbial tail, used exactly once]** `viking.md:1212`
> Finding nothing useful, he folded his legs pretzel-style and sank to the ground where he was, ignoring muddy boots and the moisture that coated his heavy kevrotex trousers.

**"Kevrotex" appears once in 92,000 words**, in the last three words of a sentence about sitting down. The purest instance of the technique in either book. **A model will not leave a word that lonely unless told to.**

**[a new noun taught by coordination with a known one]** `cordimancy.md:456`
> What about the *vikuras*, or the bandits that sometimes frequented the pass?

*Vikuras* is coordinated with "bandits," so the grammar teaches the category. When cordimancy does gloss, it is a four-word appositive, once, on first appearance, never repeated.

## What a model gets wrong

**Action rhythm, above all.** The naive move is a stack of clipped sentences inside one paragraph — *He ran. His lungs burned. The thing was closer.* He does the opposite: the physical action lives in long compound-complex sentences, and the short sentences are **isolated as one-line paragraphs** doing a specific non-action job — counting distance, marking a realization, closing a scene. **The staccato comes from paragraph breaks, not clipped syntax.** A model that shortens sentences without isolating them produces a texture he never uses. This is also exactly what went wrong in `content/superseded/`.

**Openings establish; his begin.** A model opens on weather, a room, or a name and job before letting anything happen. These open inside a force, a collapse, or a line of dialogue and let the reader assemble the situation from body sensation.

**Endings get glossed.** A model closes on summary or emotional interpretation. These stop on an implement, a two-word question, a gesture, or a countdown into absence, and never explain what the ending means. **A summarizing sentence afterward is the single thing that would destroy every restraint passage in §15.**

**Everyone speaks in complete sentences.** His characters are constantly cut off — by lungs, by rank, by someone else's better information — and the interruption carries the meaning. Attribution is over-supplied too: expect *he said grimly* on every line, where he runs fourteen turns with one re-anchoring beat.

**Subtext gets stated.** Asked for a conspiracy, a model writes characters referencing the crime; viking transacts a murder via a seating chart and a good wish. Asked for a proposition and a refusal, a model has someone say what is being offered.

**Humour fails in four specific ways.** A model writes jokes everyone enjoys — his land on someone or fail outright. A model gives comic lines to likeable characters — he gives the best timing to the bully and to the dying old man. A model writes quips — he writes *bits*, business other characters can watch. And a model lets the comedian win his own exchange, where the deadpan straight man always tops him.

**Stillness gets filled with exposition or lyricism.** He fills it with hands. Someone is always whittling, tossing a stick, rubbing fern into swollen ankles. The task supplies the beats and the silences; remove it and the scene becomes people taking turns delivering backstory into a void.

**Invented terms get explained.** The reflex is an appositive, a "what's a X?", or a paragraph of etymology. His method is to put the new noun in the *last* position of a sentence about something mundane and never return to it.

**Sensory rationing runs backwards.** A model distributes description evenly and defaults to the visual. He spends almost nothing on ordinary travel and heavily on one hinge moment, leading with smell, temperature or sound.
