# Titles and cover art

The series is **Cretaceous**. The three books are **Drift**, **Watch** and **Return** — one word each, set beneath the series word.

`[?]` marks a session proposal. Everything unmarked was settled in conversation.

## Why the set-marker is the series word and not part of the titles

A trilogy reads as a set when a fixed element repeats. The fixed element belongs **above** the title, where a four-syllable word is read once and skipped thereafter, and where it costs the individual titles nothing. Putting a second marker inside the titles pays twice for one job.

That also keeps *Cretaceous* — which is accurate, pronounceable, and does the dinosaur-promise work for the reader who wants a Cretaceous survival book — without making any single book's title a promise of dinosaurs on every page.

## What each title is doing

**Drift** is book 1's discovery. Nobody lied; the understanding decayed. It carries the mudwalk and the sea underneath it, and it is the shortest statement of the corruption the trio uncover at Genesis (`plan/outline.md` step 12, `plan/milieu-brief.md` §2).

**Watch** is book 2's instrument. Omya recites a sky she has checked for sixty years <!-- @WF-omya.watch.years: 60 --> while losing the protein fold, and the duty nobody counted as a loss becomes the dataset that dates the impact (`plan/milieu-brief.md` §5, `plan/sequels.md`). Titling the book after the thing nobody valued is the book's argument.

**Return** is book 3, three ways at once: the burial rite (`plan/milieu-brief.md` §4), the return anchor in the physics (`kb/worldbuilding/benals-equation.md`), and Cecilia coming home — the only return in the trilogy that works, while the Enclave's own answer is to go the other way and never come forward again (`plan/sequels.md`).

## Systems considered and declined

Recorded so they are not proposed again. Each failed for a stated reason, not on taste.

| system | why not |
|---|---|
| *Cretaceous* / *Maastrichtian* / *Chicxulub* | Names coordinates rather than subject — the setting is the obstacle course, not the story. Promises dinosaurs on every page. One of the three is an event, not a period. The middle term is unpronounceable outside paleontology. And the escalation runs backwards: the books get more human as they go while the titles get more technical. |
| *Creta* / *Maastri* / *Chicxu* | Truncations read as placeholders that never got replaced, and they make hard words harder — nobody knows where to stress *Maastri*. |
| *Creta Drift* / *Creta Watch* / *Creta Return* | The noun-adjunct promises a place that does not exist in the book. *Creta* is Latin and Spanish for Crete, Spanish and Italian for chalk, and a Hyundai model sold across India, Brazil and Russia. The rhythm collides with *Tokyo Drift* — same two-word shape, same stress, same second word. And nobody in this world would ever say it: the colonists do not know what era they are in and have no name for it. |
| *K-Drift* / *K-Watch* / *K-Return* | A single letter plus a hyphen is a product-code construction in English (X-Men, T-Rex, K-9, E-Type), and K- specifically now reads Korean first (K-pop, K-drama, K-beauty). It also pays twice for the set-marker, and it breaks the typography: a hyphen is a bar through a display word, and *K-Return* no longer matches *K-Drift* in width. The K–Pg idea survives as a cover device — see the boundary rule below. |
| *Genesis* / ? / *Exodus* | Genesis-to-Exodus is a two-book shape, which is why no middle term would come. *Genesis* is also already a proper noun in this world, and as a title it fits book 2's setting better than book 1's. |
| `+206` / `+207` / `-1M` | Cold on a cover, and `-1M` gives away book 3's ending. The stamp notation survives as a cover device — see the typography section. |
| *Teva's Watch* / *Keo's Heresy* / *Benal's Solution* | The strongest of the declined systems, and it fails on assignment rather than concept. Book 1 has no owner — it is the trio equally, in rotating third-person limited <!-- @WF-pov: third-limited --> — so the honest mapping is *(nobody)* / Benal / Cecilia, with a hole where book 1 goes. |
| Quantum entanglement, temporal paradox | The physics of this trilogy is deliberately unflashy, and these titles promise the space opera the book is not. |
| *ex-Enclave* / *ex-?* / *ex-??* | The Latin prefix does not scan as English, and the three losses are not parallel enough to fill the slots. |

**One title is unavailable.** *Enclave* is Ann Aguirre's 2011 YA post-apocalyptic novel, close enough in genre to be a real collision. Do not spend it on a cover.

# The covers

## What is fixed across all three

- **Dark**, and **no dinosaur in frame on any cover**.
- A **figure in jungle**, looking at something mysterious and a little dangerous.
- **The figure occupies at most a third of the frame.** Two thirds is environment.
- **The K–Pg boundary as a graphic rule.** A hairline horizontal at the same y-position on all three covers, with the tone shifting above and below it — which is what the boundary looks like in outcrop, a thin dark clay line with different rock either side. A geologist reads it instantly. Everyone else reads a design element that unifies three books. The device does its work invisibly, which is the property the `K-` prefix was reaching for and could not have, because an unexplained letter in a title has to be stepped over and a hairline does not.

`[?]` **And the figure crosses it.** Below the line on *Drift*, at it on *Watch*, above it on *Return* — a small human crossing a stratigraphic boundary across three covers, which is the trilogy. The wrinkle worth stating: book 3 does two opposite things at once, since Cecilia comes forward and the Enclave goes a million years deeper, so the rising figure reads emotionally rather than stratigraphically.

## The suit is the primary asset

From `kb/worldbuilding/safety-suits.md`: the outer layer is a living chromatophore biofilm that matches surrounding light within a second or two, across UV as well as visible, and healthy film is **faintly bioluminescent**; dead patches stay visible until they regrow. The helmet has **no faceplate, ever** — nose and mouth are open to the air in every configuration — and carries a flip-down eye shield, a single transparent curve brow to cheekbone, with the display projected on its **inner** surface. Shield up, the readout is a bioluminescent strip woven into the forearm. Twelve suits <!-- @WF-suits.remaining: 12 --> remain and each is degraded in its own way; Benal's has a red-lined shoulder capacitor.

`[?]` So a suited figure is not a silhouette against leaves. It is a person-shaped absence with the foliage running across it, betrayed by the outline being wrong and by one small honest light.

`[?]` **Which makes the forearm light load-bearing.** At a third of the frame or less, a camouflaged figure is genuinely undetectable, so the glow is what finds them for the eye rather than the outline. That also gives a strong thumbnail: a dark rectangle with a single small light in it, three times.

`[?]` The suits are **bio-functional, sleek, grounded** — not powered armor, not cyberpunk. A hard strut-reinforced skull-cap over a bare human jaw is the silhouette, and it does not resemble anything else on the shelf.

## The escalating variables

`[?]` All four point the same direction, and they run at once rather than one being chosen.

| | *Drift* | *Watch* | *Return* |
|---|---|---|---|
| figure visibility | camouflaged, nearly gone | half-present, in real light | fully lit |
| gaze | level, hunted | up, searching | through |
| sky | needle-slits in a closed canopy | a torn opening | open |
| light source | your own body | the sky | something built |

`[?]` **Environment is the primary variable**, because at a third of the frame the figure is too small to carry the read at retail size. Figure visibility is the secondary read, for someone holding the book.

`[?]` **Palette rule:** roughly ninety percent black-green on every cover, and exactly **one** saturated light, which is the light source in the table above. Book 3's is the first warm light in three books.

## Per-book briefs

### Drift

Benal, for the silhouette — 190 cm, 65 kg, tall to the point of looking wrong (`plan/milieu-brief.md` §9). The suit's technology is visible; the jungle is implied around it.

`[?]` **Mid-wide, not over the shoulder.** The two-thirds rule rules out the over-the-shoulder framing, which is figure-dominant by construction — the near shoulder and head take half the frame. Benal small and low instead, the understory towering, sightlines closing at a meter or two at chest height (`plan/milieu-brief.md` §3), depth built from receding fog planes rather than from detail.

`[?]` **The threat is a shadow, not an animal.** A pterosaur's shadow crossing three fog planes at once gives the scale without putting anything in frame, and it plants Teva's fear of the sky before she has it. The alternative is the constrictor as pure geometry — a coil crossing the frame thicker than the trunk beside it, wet-shined — which reads as *wrong shape* rather than as a species. Both avoid the over-the-shoulder-plus-looming-monster composition, which is the most-used shape in YA horror and will read as a dinosaur however it is cropped.

### Watch

A figure looking up at the stars through a cloud bank, dark jungle at the edges.

`[?]` **The lightning is inside the cloud, not a bolt.** A mass lit from within, not a bright busy line competing with a one-word title — and it keeps the internally-lit rhyme running from the suit's bioluminescence through to book 3's discharge.

`[?]` Two thirds sky. Put the figure up on a nest platform or a canopy-walk, physically higher than book 1's crouch, so vertical position climbs with everything else.

`[?]` **One object in the star field is not a star** — a faint mark with a hair-thin tail, no larger than the rest. Nobody sees it on a shelf. Anyone who finishes the book goes back and looks.

### Return

A figure before a discharge, jungle surround.

`[?]` **Not a ball of plasma**, which is the one element in the three sketches that tips toward space opera. A vertical seam of light, edge-on — a slit rather than a sphere — or the whole effect thrown behind the figure so that it is negative space and the jungle is hard cut-out with no interior detail. The emptiest of the three covers, which is right for the last one.

## Typography

`[?]` *Drift*, *Watch* and *Return* are five, five and six letters, so all three can be set at the same optical width: three near-identical word-blocks in the same position on each cover. That is what makes a set read as a set from across a table.

`[?]` **Series word above, in letterspaced caps with an archival treatment.** It says there is a real Cretaceous in here without a dinosaur having to say it.

`[?]` **The archive stamp, small and monospaced, somewhere quiet.** `A+206` on *Drift*, tying the covers to the epigraph channel's own notation (`content/epigraphs.md`). Books 2 and 3 take whatever year they land on.

`[?]` **The title goes into the environment's empty region** rather than competing with the two thirds: the canopy gap on *Drift*, the sky on *Watch*, the flat black beside the seam on *Return*. Type over dark negative space is not spending image budget.

`[?]` **The risk to watch.** Three dark covers with a small figure can drift toward quiet literary rather than YA survival thriller. The counterweight is the type doing the shouting — one word, enormous, high contrast — and the single saturated light. If the art gets subtle, the type has to get louder to compensate.

## Open

- **Whose figure on books 2 and 3.** Teva is the one who inherits the sky, and book 2 is Benal's. For book 3, an adult nobody recognizes — Cecilia — would say on the cover that the story has outgrown the three children; the alternative is discipline, the same three teenagers, and never a face on any of them.
- **Whether the boundary rule itself moves** on *Return* — breaks, or shifts above the figure — or whether only the figure moves and the rule stays fixed.
- **Whether the first warm light is right** for book 3, or whether the trilogy should end cold.
