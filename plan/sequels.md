# The sequels — shape of books 2 and 3

**This is a record, not a design.** It exists so that book 1 can plant what the later books need and can avoid closing doors they have to walk through. Nothing here is worked out at scene level and it does not need to be.

The deep past is `kb/worldbuilding/the-second-jump.md`; this is the forward half. Between them they are the whole timeline.

Marked `[?]` items are session inference, not rulings.

---

## Book 2

**Benal is badly frightened of the deadline, and he is unsatisfied by two things.**

- **The time capsule had no usable content.** It proved the second jump happened and told them nothing they could act on.
- **The equipment used to jump is missing from Genesis.** He works this out from pictures and from temporal flashes: something was there and is not. **Where did it go?**

**So he goes back to Genesis, by wig** — once the Vitarium has grown it a new power membrane from the starter cysts and the etched plate Keo carried home, without which it cannot recharge or fly back (`kb/worldbuilding/the-wig.md` §7b). His father — a committed Brancher — backs him, actively, campaigning and supporting: D19.7, where Marek says aloud that he was wrong and stands beside him, was the down payment on that change, and book 2 is where he delivers on it. `[pays R22 — the river gratings must be raised to open the channel, established at D2.5 as the reason for the drill]` **Every flight opens the croc barrier**, so a wig used routinely is a standing hole in the perimeter, and that cost is book 2's to spend.

**The schism at the Enclave gets worse.** The trio's revelation has split the society and control of Genesis and the wig is what it splits over.

**He sets up radio between the Enclave and Genesis**, and this is the enabling move for everything else. *(No conflict with `content/epigraphs.md`'s "nothing crosses, ever": that rule is about **time**. Radio across eighty kilometers <!-- @WF-route.overland: 80km --> of space is ordinary physics and always was.)*

**And it lets him make sense of what the Watch is for.** With the link open, **Omya can pass along a perfect copy of the founders' table, with Teva's help** — the moon's calendar as the founders predicted it, held unchanged for two hundred years in the memory of a woman who cannot retain a protein fold and can still recite the stars; checked against observed coverings, it dates them *(`plan/milieu-brief.md` §5)*. The knowledge nobody valued turns out to be the instrument.

`[?]` **Keo's thread is not settled.** He has graduated to the Explorers. There should be romantic tension with Teva and possibly a second interest, so confusion. One candidate occupation: searching for things the original colonists lost on the surveys and moves between Genesis and the Enclave — which would give him a reason to be out in the world, keep him on the route the first book crossed, and let him find physical evidence the others cannot.

### Book 2 ends on two things

1. **Benal proves that paradox is impossible** — pinned history, `kb/worldbuilding/benals-equation.md`: what anyone has lived is fixed, the unlived can change, and a contradicting act forks its actor away. Not that the Branchers are right; both factions were half right. A mathematical proof that there is no temporal paradox to fear. This voids the argument the entire society has been organized around for two hundred years, and voids the doctrine every taboo descends from.
2. **The Watch data dates the impact precisely: about four years away.**

**These two are `[?]` in their placement.** Both could land at the end of book 2, or one could hold to the end of book 3, and the relative order is open.

*(The arithmetic holds: book 1 leaves a window of nought to seventeen years <!-- @WF-impact.window.max: 17y -->, and four is inside it. The precision does not contradict anything — it collapses a range that was always this bad and nobody knew.)*


### Benal's road to pinned history

Benal discards theories before he settles on the true one. These are the candidates and why each falls; the order and the objections are session work, with outside models consulted on a neutral statement of the problem.

- `[?]` **Theory 0: the Branchers are right.** Every backward jump makes a branch, so nothing done here touches home. His father's faith and his own. **Falls to the capsule:** a branch opened at −1000 has no cause to contain the founders' arrival at year 0, which came from outside, so nobody would be at year 206 to dig the capsule up.
- `[?]` **Theory 1: one timeline, so the Traditionalists were right.** The capsule is an act at −1000 reaching year 206 on the same history (the morning he wakes sweating). **Falls to its consequence:** on one timeline the open emigration of book 3 is impossible unless its ruins were always in the rock, and the founders' fear would have been justified. It is despair, and he cannot make it agree with the twenty-first century's empty geology.
- `[?]` **Theory 2: entanglement runs over the whole span between arrival and origin.** A founder who arrived at 66 Mya from 2050 could stop anywhere between, which would let Cecilia come to year 206. **Falls to the founders' own plate**, whose return rule is lived time only (`kb/worldbuilding/benals-equation.md`: a founder at 66 Mya cannot go forward to 1950), and it still leaves one timeline where clues reach home.
- `[?]` **Theory 3: backward jumps select a consistent past.** Many possible pasts, and only the one consistent with the traveler's later appearance permits the jump. **Falls as fatalism:** it is Theory 1 in different words, and it forbids any act the record does not already hold.
- `[?]` **Theory 4: Genesis is a nexus where time and place entanglement combine.** It fits the scar and the flashes. **Falls to symmetry:** if standing on the scar let Cecilia reach a time she never lived, it would let the descendants reach 2050, and book 1's stranding dies.
- `[?]` **Considered and rejected, not necessarily Benal's:** contradiction-triggered branching with passengers riding a pilot's entanglement (breaks the stranding unless range-limited); branching that inherits outside arrivals (works, but needs ad hoc laws); a sealed exile history unconnected to home (works, but forbids the open emigration); Cecilia reaching 206 by stasis rather than a jump (a gimmick the reunion at −953 makes unnecessary).
- **Pinned history**, the settled answer: what anyone has lived is fixed; the unlived is one history that can change; a contradicting act forks its actor away. It keeps every theory's true part: Theory 1's one history, Theory 0's safety, Theory 4's flashes as perception rather than passage.

`[?]` **One consequence to plan for:** undoing a death the actor witnessed contradicts what they lived, so it forks them away and saves nobody in their own world. Going back to save Noli would leave Keo's Teva and Benal behind.

## Book 3

**With paradox off the table, the question stops being *may we* and becomes *how do we save everyone*.**

**Teva has become a leader.** `[?]` The arc that began as rage and became nuance in book 1 arrives here as authority.

**They find the thing that has been missing.** Benal or Keo locates the jump equipment the second jumpers took back with them, carefully buried — and **that is what the capsule was for.** Beyond the payload described in `kb/worldbuilding/the-second-jump.md`, its message was going to be: *dig this up and use it.*

**Then Cecilia.** They already know none of them can travel forward. But a clue shows that **Cecilia took the backup equipment**, and they decide to go and get her. They do: Benal meets her at −953, among the old men, and she goes home along her own worldline, by way of year 22 to the twenty-first century, with working equipment and the cure — because she is an original colonist and the worldline is hers. **She never comes forward to the Enclave's own time**, which she never lived.

**And the Enclave takes the other set and goes back a million years**, to 67 Mya, at Genesis. No paradox to fear now, so they can build openly and improve for a million years: solve the impact, or invent space travel and leave the planet.

**The book ends with Cecilia saving the twenty-first century, and the Enclavists arriving at Genesis 67 Mya to start over — fearless, and united.**

## What book 1 must therefore protect

**Two kinds of thing, held two different ways.** A **constraint** is a door book 1 must not close, and it is held by a `@WF-` claim that collides the moment some other file contradicts it. A **plant** is something book 1 sets up for a later book to pay, and it is held by a row in `plan/foreshadow-and-motif.md` §2 with a `B2` or `B3` payoff address. **The plants are not restated here** — the ledger owns them and a second copy would drift, which is what happened to the two that used to live in this list.

The constraints:

- **The capsule is not opened in book 1.** <!-- @WF-capsule.opened.book1: false --> It is dug up and carried, and what it says is book 2's opening.
- **Nothing crosses time, in either direction, ever.** <!-- @WF-timecrossing.possible: false --> Book 3's rescue is people going in person and coming back in person along their own worldlines. <!-- @WF-return.rule: own-worldline --> No signals, no messages, no exceptions.
- **The damaged temporal equipment at Genesis is a part and not the whole.** <!-- @WF-genesis.equipment.partial: true --> Book 2 turns on something being absent, so book 1 must not let what is standing there read as all of it.

The plants this book must land, claimed from the ledger:

- `[pays R9d — Teva's sky has a permanent gap, so the only complete Watch is Omya's]` Book 2's dataset has to come from Omya, with Teva's help, not from Teva alone.
- `[pays R9c — the Watch established as a duty nobody remembers the purpose of]` Omya reciting the sky perfectly while losing the protein fold is the dataset book 2 opens the radio link for.
- `[?]` **Option, not a commitment:** the impactor first shows as a new faint point in **Dead Man's Journey**, the field the reader has heard counted as nine stars since D2.4.
- `[pays R15b — Marek's dismissal shown as pity rather than contempt, and therefore reversible]` Book 2 has him supporting his son.
- `[pays R24 — fragment 8's anomaly planted, resonant, and explained by nobody]` Book 2 is where Benal connects it to the flash and the disorientation.
- `[pays R23 — Chelo named once and never explained]` Book 3 is where Marisol's daughter turns out to be who the cure comes home to.

## What became of the Lost Colonists

**Benal never finds them, and that is settled.** The mystery arc pays off through Cecilia instead, and the two figures are not in conflict: **the second jump is a thousand years <!-- @WF-secondjump.distance: 1000y -->, and 67 Mya is where the Enclave emigrates at the end of book 3 <!-- @WF-emigration.distance: 67Mya -->.** Two different jumps, a million years apart.

**They failed.** They never built the equipment they went back for, and they lived out their lives in simple subsistence. What they did manage was to **bury the capsule and the jump equipment for the other scientists to find at 66 Mya** — a message and a machine, addressed forward across a thousand years <!-- @WF-secondjump.distance: 1000y --> to people they had walked away from.

**Cecilia reaches them, and the jump is inexact.** She goes back intending a thousand years <!-- @WF-secondjump.distance: 1000y --> and travels about nine hundred and seventy-five, so she does not arrive among peers — **she arrives among old men.** She is in her fifties; they have been there four decades. She learns their whole story and is able to give them the one thing that would have made it bearable: **she found the cure.**

**And then she is stuck.** They have had setbacks and can no longer generate power, so there is no way to send her back. She lives out years there, a little younger than the people she came to find.

**Benal ends it.** He digs up the equipment they buried, uses it with power generation carried from 66 Mya, and goes back — which is the moment the capsule's whole purpose finally lands, a thousand-odd years late. He collects Cecilia and tells the scientists what they have never known: **they now have both the cure and working time-travel equipment.** They choose to stay; forty years is a life, and it is theirs. **Cecilia returns to 66 Mya, and then to the twenty-first century.**

**The capsule and the equipment are two caches, not one.** Otherwise Benal's dig in book 1 turns up the machine as well and there is no book 3. The capsule is small, findable, and is the index; the equipment is elsewhere and larger. That also explains book 2's frustration — the capsule is damaged, so the part that says *where* is exactly what is lost.

## Open questions

**Why did Cecilia not simply go home?** She is an original colonist, so the worldline is hers, and she had the proof. Instead she took the backup rig and went **backward**, to the people who had left. `[?]` The shape of an answer is visible: the only thing she could not do alone was *prove it to anyone who mattered*, and the men who had refused her the trial were a thousand years <!-- @WF-secondjump.distance: 1000y --> upstream. But it has not been decided, and the answer probably determines what kind of person she was — which reaches back into how her letters read in book 1.

**The return rule is settled and the mechanism is entanglement.** Backward travel is unrestricted; forward travel requires that you be entangled with the destination, which happens by having physically lived through it. Cecilia's entangled intervals are the twenty-first century, 66 Mya from year 0 to 22, and the deep past from −975 to −953. The anchor is the departure event rather than the arrival instant, so she boards the leg home from year 22. Full statement in `kb/worldbuilding/benals-equation.md`. **It also explains the book 3 emigration without a special case:** the Enclave going back a million years is backward travel and needs nothing, and those who lived at 66 Mya can come forward only to the years they lived there, which the impact ends, and their children born at 67 Mya never can, which is fine because they are not planning to.

**What a jump costs, and what the sequels must reckon with.** The rule is in `kb/worldbuilding/benals-equation.md`, *What a jump costs*: about a hundred kilowatt-hours per person, nearly regardless of depth, paid where each leg starts, because energy cannot be sent through time. These figures are orders of magnitude, never numbers on the page. Consequences any sequel has to face:
- **The bank is gone.** The founders' banked energy went partly with the stayers and the rest leaked away over two centuries. The only source at Genesis is the generators, at about four hundred watts <!-- @WF-genesis.charge.spare: 400W --> spare.
- **Every return leg carries its own energy.** Whoever goes back must take the energy for coming forward with them, as stored charge, and its weight rides in the jump too. Cecilia's own solo jump spent one person's share of the bank; how she meant to pay for her return is part of the open question above.
- **Benal fetching Cecilia** costs a share for him going out at 66 Mya, and two shares at the deep-past end for both of them coming back, which he has to carry there.
- **Book 3's emigration does not fit on the generators.** About nine hundred people <!-- @WF-population: 900-1000 --> need some ninety megawatt-hours. At four hundred watts <!-- @WF-genesis.charge.spare: 400W --> that is about twenty-five years, and the impact is at most seventeen <!-- @WF-impact.window.max: 17y --> away. The founders' grown solar membrane, which book 2 regrows from the cysts and the etched recipe (`kb/worldbuilding/the-wig.md` §2), is the obvious second source, and the arithmetic of the two together is a clock book 3 can run on.
- **Short hops cost what long ones do**, so going back a week to undo a death is possible in principle once anyone has a working rig and a bank. **It would never be tolerated.** Time travel was discovered very shortly before the pandemic, and although its mechanics were worked out, nobody ever proved whether a change in the past can cancel a future (`plan/milieu-brief.md` §1). Chicxulub was chosen because it was the one place that guaranteed, as best they could imagine, no anomaly risk, and leaving no trace is the culture's first law because of it. Undoing Noli's death, or interfering with the past in any other way, is the one act nobody in either book's culture would countenance, whatever it cost them not to.

**What is at Genesis, then?** `plan/milieu-brief.md` §3 lists "the damaged temporal equipment" as still there in book 1. `[?]` The reconciliation: the second jumpers took the **return rig** with them, which is how they jumped and why it is buried a thousand years <!-- @WF-secondjump.distance: 1000y --> upstream, while what remains at Genesis is the damaged **arrival** apparatus. **Two devices, not one.** <!-- @WF-genesis.equipment.partial: true -->
