# Voice sheets

Planning layer B12. Per-character idiolect, **derived from the canon prose rather than invented**, so these are descriptions of what is already on the page and not a new set of rules to violate.

The failure this exists to prevent: every character sounding like the same competent narrator. A line that any of them could have said is a line none of them should say.

---

## The three

### Keo

**Shape.** Shortest sentences in the book. Imperatives under stress, often stacked with no connective: *"Drink. Both of you. And empty. Now. Before the suits."* Practical instruction is his default mode of caring, and it is the only way he knows how to be kind.

**Tics.** *"We'll be fine."* Calls Benal **Ben** when they are close and **Benal** when they are not — the switch is a thermometer and should be used as one. Trails into ellipsis when emotional and then abandons the sentence rather than finishing it.

**Never says.** That he is frightened. Anything about Yara. Anything about Teva.

**Answers** directly, and too fast.

**Funny about:** other people's misfortune, affectionately. Bravado as deflection. **This register dies at the midpoint and must not come back** — after 7.2 his jokes should fail to land or fail to finish.

### Teva

**Shape.** Extreme compression. One- and two-word answers as a default: *"I know." "Let them talk." "I am." "Genesis." "Suicide."* She does not elaborate unless she is angry, and when she does elaborate it is a **recitation** — marshalled evidence delivered in order, which the prose has already flagged as sounding *"like a Keeper reciting a protocol."*

**Tics.** Commands rather than requests. Present tense. Names the thing and stops.

**Never says.** Anything about her own body — not tired, not cold, not hurt, not afraid. Hold this hard — it is how the reader tracks her decline, because it can only be shown from outside.

**Answers** with an instruction, or with the shortest true thing.

**Funny about:** almost nothing, which is the point. Her rate is roughly one joke to Benal's six. When she does land one it is flat, hostile and dry — *"Be quiet or be bait. Your choice."* — and it should surprise everyone including her.

### Benal

**Shape.** The longest sentences of the three, and the only subordinate clauses. Technical nouns in lists. Hedges everything that is not a fact: *I think*, *probably*, *maybe three*.

**The tell, and it is already in the canon.** He stammers when frightened — *"I... I heard you. I was... waiting."* — and **the stammer vanishes completely the moment he is on his own subject**: *"It's temporal anchoring. It's drift mechanics. It's the reason our ancestors couldn't just jump back."* Fluency is the signal that he has stopped being afraid, and it is the fastest way to show him growing.

**Never says.** That he needs help. That the pity hurts.

**Answers** with data, and with rhetorical questions that are really arguments — *"Will you use your magnetic sense to navigate a command prompt?"*

**Funny about:** death, immediately, in the flattest possible register, and **he does not know he is being funny.** *"Sky or ground. Pick your death."* He carries the book. See `plan/humour-plan.md`.

## The adults

**Joram** — heavy contraction, minimal: *"S'nothing. G'back to sleep, Ri."* Says less than he means and expects to be understood. Declarative. Never explains twice.

**Riel** — **counts.** Lists things, folds fingers down one at a time, gives quantities where another character would give an impression. Her precision is affection.

**Omya** — two voices, and the gap between them is the tragedy. **Lucid:** aphoristic, short absolute declaratives in sequence — *"Precision, Teva. We are scientists, not shamans. This work is not magic. It is knowledge. And knowledge must be perfect."* **Lost:** the technical vocabulary is replaced by **food words**. She offers a vector and asks about bread. Canon at `content/superseded/01.1.md:53`, and it is the exact mechanism to reuse in 2.4.

**Rano** — repeats himself under pressure, and uses full nomenclature while pleading, which is what makes him pitiable: *"The rust-red. Position one-oh-seven. The final fold protocol. You know this."*

**Marek** — rhetorical, escalating, and **the only adult who reaches for metaphor**: *"It's like a basket of pan, and we are starving because we are afraid to lift the lid."* Public register even at home, which is precisely why his son cannot hear him.

**Sila** — first person plural, always. *We are a seed.* Doctrine, and the antithesis construction: *better X than Y*. **She never says "I."**

**Hesh** — arithmetic. Rhetorical questions that are arguments with numbers in them. The prose already has it: he taps each word like a chisel.

**Elana** — anaphora. Repetition as grief, in threes: *"She did not die from a croc. She did not fall from a nest."*

## Speech that is not spoken aloud

Cretaceous has three channels of this — Keo's projection into animals, the proximity sense, and the AI at Genesis — and none of them had a convention. Viking solves it **typographically, and does not narrate it at all.**

**Italics, no quotation marks, no attribution tag.** Never *he projected*, never *she transmitted*, never *the AI said in his mind*. The typography does the work the tag would otherwise do, and the absence of a tag is what makes it read as a different channel rather than as decorated speech.

**The two-channel exchange is the trick worth stealing for the AI.** One speaker in quotes, one in italics, alternating, with no narration between them:

> *You may not know this, but I've been following you for a while*, Geire began.
> "How's that?"
> *Well, MEEGO's been up to no good for years…*
> "So I was a spy."
> *You were.*

The rhythm is deliberately asymmetric — the mediated voice gets long fluent paragraphs and flat one-word confirmations; the aloud voice gets short statements that aren't even questions and get answered anyway. **That is exactly the shape a conversation between an exhausted boy and a maintenance system should have.**

**One-way channels use paragraph breaks as the beats where someone would have interrupted**, and the reaction is deferred to prose afterward rather than interrupting the transmission.

**Non-human minds are made alien by substituted nouns inside plain syntax, not by weird grammar.** Viking's aliens speak prim, almost formal English and call humans *earthbounds* and a satellite *the siren in The Cold*. The strangeness is entirely in what gets named. **For Noli this means her projected register is simple and concrete** — *good-pack-good-food* is already right — and her opinions arrive as odd category judgments rather than broken language.

**And beings without faces get their emotional beats rendered as body state.** Viking uses color: *a yellow blush of amusement, cherry surprise*. Noli's equivalent is feather state, posture, the sickle claw, the set of her tail — established once and then used as punctuation.

## Two rules from the dialogue exemplars

**1. Nobody explains their own cleverness.** In viking a good idea arrives as a *question*, gets resisted, and then **the recognition happens on the other person's face** — "Understanding flooded across Heward's face." The clever character never gets to lay it out.

**This matters more for Benal than for anyone**, because his arc is being right, and a boy who narrates his own correctness is insufferable. **He proposes in the interrogative and somebody else's face does the rest.** Note also that viking follows the winning idea with "Nobody looked at Rafa" — the credit is withheld.

**2. Physiology costs a character the floor.** Whemper is cut off mid-argument by his own lungs, somebody else takes the floor, and he has to fight his way back in. The body business is placed *after* the line, so it reads as the price of having spoken. **Benal's chest after the constrictor, Keo's fever on Day 9, and Teva's failing breath on Day 7 should all do this** — not as description, but as something that costs them an argument.

## Questions each scene refuses to answer

From the viking dossier: Rafa refuses to describe his wife — *"It's not the sort of topic that lends itself to a few choice adjectives"* — and the final line withholds in the opposite direction. **Models answer every question a scene raises.** So every scene brief carries a short list of what it will not answer.

Standing refusals for this novel:

- **What Yara was like.** She gets facts and never a portrait.
- **Whether Teva knows how Keo feels.** Never confirmed from either side.
- **What Joram was afraid of**, until 14.1, and then only by implication.
- **Whether Omya knows what is happening to her.** One moment where she might, and no confirmation.
- **What the flashes are**, until 12.1 — and *what the Lost Colonists became*, ever.
- **Whether the founders were right.** The book takes no position.

## Rules

1. **Attribution is `said`, or an action beat.** viking runs 111 *said* against a single *replied*. Exotic tags are rationed to the low tens across a whole novel.
2. **Indirect answers at plot-critical moments are the norm**, not a flourish. A character answering the question they were asked is the exception.
3. **Register is a thermometer.** Benal's stammer, Keo's *Ben*/*Benal*, Teva's word count. The reader tracks these without knowing it.
4. **Nonce verb coinages in dialogue are encouraged and must fire exactly once** — see `kb/worldbuilding/lingo.md` §4. A coinage that recurs has become a dialect word, which is the failure the cordimancy dossier warns about.
5. **Nobody here is articulate about their own feelings.** An exception is something to be earned rather than reached for. The nearest anyone comes is 10.1, and even there it comes out sideways.
