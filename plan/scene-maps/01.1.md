IN     the Vitarium, evening of Day 1, two to three weeks into the crisis

       world facts, so nothing here gets invented at the keyboard:
         a recessed chamber off the main kiva, entered through a natural
         cleft. Limestone. Constant temperature, high humidity, no daylight.
         Lit only by the cultures themselves. Dozens of lopsided clay
         vessels in clefts in the rock, each shaped to mimic a random
         clump so that nothing looks made. One vessel, one cell line,
         one vector. Healthy cultures glow and the color is the
         diagnostic: rust-red is T-cell activation, blue is lysine and
         digestion. Cultures are fed daily on synthetic nutrient compound
         or they apoptose inside 24-48 hours. Protein-gel sequencing keys
         hold their shape only in this chamber's humidity.
         The main kiva next door is a large dissolution cave with two
         openings (Daniel, 2026-09-10): a sloping cleft you walk down,
         hung with a ceratops hide, and a collapse hole ten to fifteen
         meters up that vents two centuries of cooking smoke and drops
         one shaft of daylight onto a talus cone. So the main kiva has
         failing evening light in it and the Vitarium has none, because
         it is a deep cleft off the side.
         Outside and above: overcast, dead still, no rain, hot and close,
         and the light going. Nothing outside acknowledges any of this.

       blocking, fixed, one room and one doorway:
         Omya at the sequencing tray, center. Rano beside her.
         Elana on the floor near the cleft with Alira. Teva between,
         nearer Omya. The elders in the main kiva, visible through the
         cleft, and not coming through it.

       Teva, 16, POV. Unhurt. Granddaughter of the last Master Keeper,
         and expected better upgrades than she got. Sensory upgrades
         null; magnetic sense and proprioception excellent. Knows
         fragments of the Keeper work — enough to read this room and
         not enough to fix it.
         fears  that nothing could have been done — that Alira just
           died the way her mother just died, and nobody could have
           stopped either
         so believes  the adults chose this. The paralysis is a
           decision, not an incapacity. If there is a culprit there
           is a lever
         so wants  someone to blame
         hides  that she is not sure she can do it
         never says anything about her own body

         (Locked by Daniel, 2026-09-10. One mechanism, not four labels:
          the fear is what makes the want necessary, and 14.1 is the
          fear coming true — she looks for choice in those faces and
          finds terror — which is rage → nuance with something under
          it. "Fears becoming them" moves to Day 9, where she has
          made a decision and been wrong.)

         And she is cold. Limestone, no suit, and thermal regulation
         that is poor in both directions — so this is where the reader
         first sees the half of it the climate never tests again until
         Day 12. She will not mention it. Show it from outside.

       Omya, last Master Keeper, dementia. Two voices and the gap
         between them is the tragedy. Lucid: short absolute declaratives.
         Lost: the technical vocabulary is replaced by food words.
         wants  to finish the sequence, which she believes she is doing
         scope note: the food substitution fires ONCE, at beat 9.
           It is a mechanism, not a manner of speaking.

       Rano, journeyman Keeper. Holds eighty percent of a process where
         eighty percent is worth nothing. Kind, and worn through.
         Repeats himself under pressure; uses full nomenclature while
         pleading, which is what makes him pitiable.
         wants  to get the rust-red out of her tonight

       Elana, Alira's mother. Came in an hour ago and has not left.
         wants  to be told it can still be done

       Alira, two and a half. Named, therefore a person, therefore a
         death the community must count. Stingburn on the wrist, the
         line risen up the arm.
         she has stopped wanting anything and is working at breathing

       the elders, in the main kiva, unnamed and not furniture.
         want  not to be the one who says it is over
         one of them will move as if to speak and will not. That is the
