## IN

- **`PLACE`** — **the narrows at low water** *(R16; the old water marks on the stones well above today's level, D7.2, D8.1)*: **a ragged line of boulders across the channel that steps downstream as it goes, so the way across is a long diagonal from stone to stone, rougher and more broken than a straight line and more manageable for it**: shallow rocky water between most of them, waist- to chest-deep, quick, over stones you can feel with your feet; and **two deep chutes where the current runs fast between boulders a few body lengths apart, with no bottom, and carries whatever is in it onto the next stone downstream** `[-]` *(see L1; the shape of a boulder riffle at low water — the river's own push decides where each chute lands you, and the downstream drift takes them further from the gathering)*. **Nobody needs a heading**: the goal is the other side alive. **The slow wide pool fifty yards upstream** *(their measure: "fifty paces, more", D7.2)*, where the decoy goes in. **Keo kept the herd all morning from a cut bank above the pool** *(D8.1)*, **and comes down to the narrows for the push.** **The far bank**: slick black mud, then a steep rise to the trees *(D8.3)*. **One word per place:** *the narrows*, *the chute*, *the pool*.
- **`TIME`** — Day 8, **about 1400**. **They cross together, all three at once, inside the window the decoy buys** — the minutes the crocs are busy with it *(CLOCK)*. **The crossing is perhaps ten minutes.** Blood reaches the narrows in **the time it takes slow water to come fifty yards — a minute or two** `[-]`.
- **`WEATHER`** — still, hot, cloudless; **one breath of wind, once** *(§2)*.
- **`LIGHT`** — the brightest hour; **she can see the bottom in the shallows and nothing in the chutes**.
- **`SOUND`** — **the decoy bellowing**; **the herd on the bank above the pool, crowding into the shallows in panic** *(the churn, §2)*; the narrows; her own breath.
- **`SMELL`** — the gel on her neck and hands; river; **blood**, later.
- **`CLOCK`** — **how long the crocs are busy with the decoy.**
- **`CAPABILITIES`** — **the suit and water — L1.** **Her proprioception is balance**: on wet rock in a current, braced, hauling a cord, her body knows where it is and stays on the stone. That is why she goes first on the line. **The croc's surface sense, and its other senses** *(D2.5; `tech-rules.md`)*. **Keo's projection**, upstream: **a push, not a hold** — the animal wants to drink, he sends it deeper than it wants to go, and then it is too late for it *(`tech-rules.md`: nudges inclination)*; **he feels its terror while he pushes** *(§9)*.
- **`LOAD`** — packs tied tight, weapons lashed to them *(D8.1)*.
- **`PRESENT`** — **all three in the water together, tied on Keo's fiber cord** *(`body-and-resources.md` §0)*: **Teva first, Benal in the middle, Keo last on his blown-up skins** *(L2)* — **Keo ties the two of them in, several body lengths apart, with a knot whose tail pulls free and which he makes Teva try**, and **holds his own end in his hand instead of tying in**, so that if something takes him he can let go and not drag them under; he does not say why, and she sees him do it; **Noli on the top of the far bank, calling**.
- **`BODY`** — **Teva:** terror of water *(`character-arcs.md`)*. **Benal:** chest sore; **elbow seal compromised and mending** *(D5.2, D6.1)*. **Keo:** the projection headache at its worst *(§3)*.

- **`RELATIONS`** — `character-arcs.md` §3, Day 8: **Teva→Benal at 7, she hauls him out.** Talk is spent only where the surface allows, because any break in it is the danger; **reeling Benal onto each stone is her care.** **Keo↔Teva**: he pushes the decoy in from the first stone, and holds his own end of the cord untied, while she takes Ben across, and she sees him still in the water at the close.

## MOVES

4. **[spine] [turn — TEVA] [ledger]** **The first chute.** — *L1. **No bottom.** **The suit holds them up, enough and no more**, and **they let the current take them across the few body lengths of fast water onto the next boulder downstream** — **you do not swim a chute; it puts you where it puts you, and you grab.** **She goes first, gets the stone, braces, and hauls the cord in hand over hand until Benal is on the rock beside her**, before the current can carry him past it. **She whispers at the stone: *don't kick; hold on; I've got you*** — talk in the water is whispered and necessary, and it is talk. ## Work queue, found by walking the invariants

- **Canon premise run.** *A boulder narrows at low water*: a riffle of boulders with shallow water between most and a few deep fast chutes is the ordinary shape of a rocky constriction at low flow. *Letting a chute carry you onto the next rock*: what non-swimmers can do if something holds them up. *Small ripples swept downstream*: D7.2. *Blood reaching the narrows*: slow water moving fifty yards in a minute or two is under a meter a second. *A hadrosaur wading in to drink and cool in heat*: ordinary; the nudge is toward what it wants anyway *(`tech-rules.md`: nudges inclination)*. *The push*: projection nudges inclination; a hadrosaur that wants to drink can be nudged deeper than it would go, and the crocs do the rest *(§3)*; the push costs him the animal's terror *(§9)*. *The croc's other senses*: they are not hidden, only quieter than the pool; the brush is a croc going past them to the louder thing. Passes.
- **What L1 commits the book to.** If the suits float a wearer, **Day 14–15's immersion at sea** *(`body-and-resources.md` §3b: immersion; the sea takes the fieldpack)* happens to people the suits hold up, and Keo, unsuited, is the one at risk. If they do not, this crossing needs a different means.
