# Tech rules — taught before exploited

Planning layer B5. Every capability in the book audited for **where its rule is taught** and **where the rule is exploited**, plus its cost.

The rule this enforces: **a capability that first appears when it is needed reads as convenience.** It must be established earlier, cost-free, in a scene where nothing depends on it — and the establishing scene must also establish the *limit*, because a power the reader cannot see the edge of removes all jeopardy.

Source of truth for the capabilities themselves: `kb/worldbuilding/safety-suits.md` and `plan/milieu-brief.md` §7.

## The four gaps, and where to close them

**Accelerated healing and its cost.** Teach it in **⟪2.3**, in the family nest: an old injury of Joram's that healed in days and left him hollow for a fortnight, mentioned in passing while he works. Establishes the rule, establishes the price, and characterizes both parents. Costs four lines.

**Suit thermal management.** Teach it in **⟪4.3**, when the suits seal — Teva's first sensation should be *cold*, startling and delicious, because the suit's whole job in this climate is to take heat away. Then Day 10 is the same sensation failing. One sentence at the theft buys the entire savanna sequence.

**Croc surface sensing.** Teach it in **⟪2.5**, the yazhi drill — this is exactly what that scene is for, and it currently only gestures at protocols. The drill is in a pool, so teach it on water: have Teva drop something in at one end and make a child watch the ring travel, or hold a hand flat on the surface and feel another child's kick from across the pool. **What the animal reads is the water, not the bank** — pressure receptors in the jaw skin, which is why the buddy rule exists and why a still river is the dangerous one. Said once by a sixteen-year-old to a four-year-old, and the reader carries it to Day 8.

*(The corpus previously had this as "reads a four-micrometer vibration" through the ground, taught by making a child feel her own footsteps in the dirt. That was a misreading of Leitch and Catania, who recorded neural responses to a probe indenting modern crocodilian skin by about 3.9 micrometers — a local touch threshold mimicking ripples striking the scales, not ground-displacement detection, and not a measurement from any extinct animal. The correction is an improvement: the crossing happens in water, so the sense that matters is the one the crossing actually trips.)*

**Suit feeding.** One moment in Act 1, ideally at ⟪4.3 — somebody gives a suit water before drinking.

**Note what these have in common.** All four gaps are closed in Act 1, three of them in scenes that already exist, and each closure also does character work. That is the test for a teaching scene: if it is only teaching, it is exposition.

## Rules

1. **A capability appearing for the first time at the moment it is needed reads as convenience.** Establish it earlier wherever the book gives you room.
2. **Teach the limit with the power.** The reader needs to see the edge, or there is no jeopardy.
3. **Every capability costs.** Projection costs pain. Healing costs strength. Camouflage costs stillness. Suits cost sugar. A free capability is a hole in the story.
4. **Cost is paid on the page, in the same scene or the next one.** Not asserted afterwards.
5. **Capabilities degrade and do not recover** — see `body-and-resources.md` §3b. The trajectory is from technology toward competence, and it is the arc of the whole book.
