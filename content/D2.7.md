The first person out pushed the hide back, and the room came out ahead of them.

Benal smelled it before he saw anyone. It was the air of a whole room of wet people shut in stone for an evening, wet hair and wet fiber cloth and breath and stale sweat, and the hide had been holding it in all that time. It rolled over him where he stood against the rock beside the cleft. He had heard the room getting up through the hide before that: feet scuffing stone, voices coming over one another. Under it all, faint and acrid, was the pulp still on his own legs from the basin.

He had come down late. The batch had taken until the light went, and left him slowed by fatigue. When he reached the kiva people were already shouting, so that what came out through the hide to him was not an argument. It was noise with his father's voice inside it, loud and then louder.

Then Elana had come past him. She had not seen him. When she went in, the shouting died away in a ragged wave, and her words reached him through the hide. After that the shouting did not come back. Now the room was emptying.

So the air had not killed anyone. While he waited, Benal had been working out how long a room that full and that shut would take to do it, and he had decided the Council would adjourn first. It usually did.

People came out past him in ones and twos. They were quiet and tired and still damp, and they did not stop. One or two of them looked at him as they went by, the way people looked at Marek's son, and Benal knew that look. It was the one that said *poor thing*. He held still for it, as he always did.

His parents came out late, after most of the room. Benal heard someone speak to his father on the way out, and heard no answer. He would have known his father's step on any platform in the Enclave. The step that came toward the hide now was slow and uneven, and he did not know it until the hide moved and they were there: his mother with her arm through Marek's, and Marek walking as though the ground had gone soft under him.

Benal had never seen him like that. At home Marek argued in the same voice he used to the Council, and let Benal finish every sentence, however long it ran, before he answered something else. His father's face was gray in the last of the dusk. His mouth was shut, and he was not looking at anyone. He looked like a man who had shouted himself out in front of the whole Enclave and lost.

*Do something real.*

Marek's words came back to Benal before he could stop them, with a satisfaction he was ashamed to feel. So that was what doing something real got you. You shouted in front of everyone until your voice went, and they let you.

Marek saw him. He stopped.

For a moment Benal thought he was going to walk on. Then his father put out a hand, not quite to Benal's shoulder, and let it drop.

"Go home, Benal." His voice came out hoarse, nearly gone. "You've been on your feet all afternoon. You'll be ill."

Even now, his father had looked at him and seen only a body that might break.

"I'm not ill," Benal said. "The batch is in. I trod it to the last count, and then I came down." It came out sounding like a report. "You're the one with no voice left. You should possibly go home first."

Marek opened his mouth to argue it, and no voice came.

Lunet looked at Benal. Her face was tired and plain.

"He was arguing for you in there," she said. "For all of us. That's what that was."

It was what a mother would say.

"He was arguing," Benal said to the ground. "I heard that much. I think the whole Enclave heard that much."

Lunet waited a moment longer. Then she turned Marek toward the nests, and they went, slowly, her arm still through his.

Benal did not follow them. He stayed where he was, against the rock beside the hide, in the dark that had come down while he wasn't watching, with the room still emptying behind him.
