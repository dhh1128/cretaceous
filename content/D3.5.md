The fire had burned down to a red seam, and the others were asleep.

Teva sat with her back to the rock and the spike across her knees, and listened to them. Keo slept the way he did everything, all at once and completely. Benal slept curled toward the coals, and twice he said something in his sleep that was not a word. Past the fence the night went about its business. Once something heavy moved through the ferns outside the stakes, stopped at the smell of them, and went on. She sat very still until she could not hear it any more.

There was no moon. It would not come up for hours yet. Through the gap in the crowns overhead the sky was thick with stars, more than she had ever seen from the Enclave, where there was always a nest or a crown or the glow of the kiva in the way.

*Was the sky watched last night?*

Nobody had watched it. Tonight she was the only one awake to do it.

She started where Omya always started. The Organs, nine: the bright pair at the foot, the five along the bend, the two that are hardly there. She found the bright pair at once, and the bend. The faintest of the nine sat a finger's width above it, Omya said, and on a hazy night you would not see it, so you counted it anyway. Teva could not see it on any night. She counted it anyway.

Trinity, four. Three she could see; the fourth was between them, for good eyes only, and she gave it its place and its number without looking for it.

Then the field after that, and the one after that. Her lips moved on the counts. It came easily, the way the first positions of the protocol always did, a long way, further than she had ever needed it to go. It came the way Omya's voice had come through her hands while she plaited, even and sure, one field and then the next.

After the sixth field there was nothing.

She waited. She knew there was a field there. She could feel the shape of the place where it ought to be, the way you can feel a step in the dark that is not where your foot expects. It did not come.

She went back to the beginning. The Organs, nine. Trinity, four. She went faster this time, the counts falling over each other, because if she came at it with enough speed she would carry straight through. She came to the end of the sixth field and stopped, at the same place, as if she had walked into a wall.

She did not try a third time.

It was not that she had forgotten it. You could not forget a thing you had never had. Omya had given it to her a hundred nights, in the same even voice, and somewhere past the sixth field Teva had always let her go on alone, because it was the useless half, and there would always be tomorrow night, and Omya would always be there to say it again. The rest of the sky was still in her grandmother's head, all of it, exactly. It was going out of there a little more every day. No one else living had it.

Teva sat with the spike across her knees and looked up at the fields she had, and kept watching them. There was nothing new in them that she could see. She did not know whether that meant anything. She watched anyway.

When her watch was done she went across the ring and put her hand on Benal's shoulder. He woke all at once, frightened, and then saw it was her.

"Your watch," she said.

She lay down by the coals with her back to them both, and did not tell him anything.
