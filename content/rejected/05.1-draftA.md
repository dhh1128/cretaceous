## 5.1

Benal stood rigid, his spine pressed against the moss-covered buttress root of a laurel whose trunk rose into the canopy. The razor-club felt useless in his hands. Its hardwood was meant for swinging, for impact, not for silent, desperate defense.

His suit suppressed the ambient insect drone. The fog and pre-dawn gloom blinded him. The hunt was scent and sound.

Click-click-chitter. The clatter was nearby.

STAY. FOCUS. Keo’s mental command was a thread of purpose that bypassed Benal’s ears. It held Noli still. The bambiraptor’s mind was a panicked, caged tremor against Keo’s control.

Benal’s keen smell tracked the hunters, cross-referencing with a chemical sensor on his wrist. The air hung dead. Then it twisted, reflecting the jungle's shifting currents. He tracked the rankness of their pheromone barrier blowing back from the north, then the raptors' musk from the east. The suit's vibration sensors struggled to localize predatory footsteps.

He subvocalized, barely moving his lips. “Scent warps. Confused.”

Teva’s subvocalized reply was a whisper. “Direction?”

“Shifts. Weakly south-east. Wary, not scared.”

They held the position for what felt like a quarter-hour. Drops of sweat trickled between Benal's shoulder blades. He squeezed his fingers on the razor-club, trying to decide if the shadowy stir in the fog was a branch or a tail. His legs burned and cramped.

*Why are they hesitating?*

Benal cycled frantically through the data. The raptors were drawn to the carrion scent but repelled by the alpha component of the pheromone -- a defense refined over two hundred years. But their suits had made the scent move. Was that interesting enough to overcome their normal aversion?

Would the raptors be targeting all three of them, or just one? If one, would it be him?

Keo would make more sense. He was unsuited, probably looked easy to damage, and had no odd suit smell. If they estimated strength by height, Keo would look better.

How did he feel that he was hoping his logic was right?

He thought about his pack. He could throw some pan to send an interesting scent into the bushes, but would they care? Or he could use the chemical pods. A bit of noise, then a scent muddle. Would he need those pods tomorrow, and regret using them prematurely today?

A sliver of moon had cleared the canopy, adding some light. Keo, using his tetrachromat vision, gazed into the fog, which seemed to be thinning. His concentration was absolute.

Teva, meanwhile, was the anchor. Her spike held steady. She projected nothing but resolve. Was she focusing her magnetic sense on south, back to the border tangle? Or north, into more of this hell?

Click... shhh... click. The sound was definitely receding now.

Keo leaned down, giving Noli a mental prod. Benal felt it. GO. DISTRACTION. NOW. The command seemed to focus distance, not panic, using her fear of the pheromone to propel her quietly away from the hollow.

Noli trembled, melting into the jungle. Her movement was slow and silent. Her retreat was the only movement Keo's proximity sense registered moving away from them.

Below, the clicking faded. Benal felt the raptors' musk reek soften to a smudge on the breeze.

He released a controlled breath. “Fading. Deterrent holding. Noli worked.”

“Wait,” Teva commanded, her voice flat. “Wait until the scent is gone.”

The silence tightened around them.

The raptors' musky reek slammed into him again. It was stronger, a clear vector from the south-east. And beneath it, the smell of freshly turned soil. *They found our tracks.*

Click-click-chitter! They were closer now, no longer circling.

“Back,” Benal hissed. “Found the line. South-east. We failed.”

Keo, who had been waiting on the branch, sprang into action. He needed to create an immediate, high-value diversion. He snatched a sharp-edged piece of limestone from the moss-gathered bark. He threw the rock in a high arc. It struck the ground behind a clump of ferns with a stomach-turning thud-squelch that signaled fresh prey.

The raptors hesitated.

Keo used the pause. He slammed his mind outward, not at the raptors, but at a grounder he sensed lurking nearby. RUN. PANIC! NOW!

The grounder, a rabbit-sized mammal, shot out into the open.

The three raptors pivoted, their instinct for easier prey overriding the high-risk scent. They vanished into the fog with a rush of feathers and a chorus of chitters.

The resulting silence was absolute.

Keo slumped onto the branch, the pressure behind his eyes an iron clutch. He began the agonizing descent.

Teva lowered her spike. Her cheeks were a mottled pink in the cold light.

Benal gasping, wheezed and retrieved his stone.

Teva turned to both of them, speaking softly on the external mic. "Your analysis of the pheromone as a tactical hesitation was correct, Benal. That was what bought us the time to deploy. Keo, the timing on the grounder was precise. It took all of us to sell the panic." She gave a decisive nod to Keo. "I chose well. Both of you."

Keo, exhausted but grateful, put a hand on Benal’s shoulder. “You saved us, Benal. I was just the megaphone for the plan you built.” He smiled, the bravado replaced by honest weariness. "Good thing you didn't throw those dried crawlers, huh?"

Benal felt the shame of his earlier panic dissolve in the wave of relief and the uncomplicated pride of being needed. His body still shook, but he felt his mind clear. “Move south-east. Against the wind. We follow the wind line back to the coast.”

Teva nodded, taking point. Benal, shoulders back, silently followed. His mind, the most important tool they had, felt keen and purposeful.
