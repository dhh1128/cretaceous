The passage let them out into the chamber, and Keo reached for the room before he had thought to. It came back as one blur, every mind in the room pressed together with no edge to any of them, and he could not find Benal in it, or Teva. He looked instead.

People sat shoulder to shoulder on the floor and stood packed behind them, and the watchers took the three of them down the middle while the crowd drew in its knees and feet to let them by. The talk went low as they passed and came up again behind them. After the insect wall at the river, so many people breathing and shifting under stone pressed on his ears. A woman drew her yazhi in against her legs, away from the starved gray skin of Teva's suit.

"Look at them. There's nothing left of them."

The shaft from the hole in the roof had gone out with the evening, and the light came from the lightmold grown into the bare walls, yellow-green and steady, the same on every face.

Joram and Riel stood against the side wall, with a watcher at each shoulder. Keo saw that much at the edge of his eye and did not turn his head.

The watchers stopped them below the dais. Sila sat at its center with her hands open on her knees, and Hesh sat beside her. In the crowd near the front, Elana stood with her arms at her sides and looked at the three of them.

"The Keepers have what you brought?" Hesh said.

"Rano has it," Teva said. "He's working."

"Then that is the Keepers' business, and they are about it," Sila said. "This is ours."

That was all the room had to say about the protocols. At the end of the passage behind them, Rano would be bent over the trays.

"You went to Genesis," Sila said. "Against the word of this Council. Tell it what you found there."

Benal stepped forward. He had the founders' tablet in both hands and the cylinder under his arm, and Keo went with him and stood at his shoulder.

"I need to show you this first."

He held the tablet up. The lightmold ran along the edge of the metal and showed the marks cut into it, and every eye in the room went to it and stayed there.

"This is from the room the founders worked in at Genesis. It's metal, like the protocol plates, and it has their stamp in the corner, the same stamp. Thirty-three months after the jump. <!-- @WF-plates.stamp: +33mo --> Year three."

He did not stammer. His voice went to the back of the room and he did not have to raise it to get it there. Benal was strong. It was a fact about him, like his height.

On the dais Hesh leaned forward.

"Whose hand is it?"

"Theirs. The same hand as the plates Rano is reading now."

"Read it."

"*Impact, narrowed down to sometime in the next two hundred and twenty years.*" <!-- @WF-dating.fix.margin: 220y -->

"But the founders told us centuries!" It came from the crowd behind Keo, a man's voice.

"The founders brought that number with them," Benal said. "They didn't know when they'd landed, not closely enough to know how long they had, so they kept working at it, and three years in <!-- @WF-dating.fix.year: 3 --> they had this one. It's the one after. Nobody at home ever had it."

"And we are to take that from a plate three children carried out of Genesis," Sila said.

"You don't have to take it from me. What we brought of their records is tied down behind Teva's seat, and the seat is in the wig, and the wig is in the river." Heads turned toward the cleft, and back. "Everyone in this room just went down there and looked at it. We didn't make that. We came home in it."

He lowered the tablet and held it against his chest.

"The next two hundred and twenty years from when they cut it, and they cut it in year three. That's the stamp. Two hundred and twenty from year three is two hundred and twenty-three. This is two hundred and six." <!-- @WF-transit.elapsed: 206y --> <!-- @WF-dating.fix.margin: 220y -->

He stopped there long enough for the room to do it with him.

"Seventeen years. At the most." <!-- @WF-impact.window.max: 17y -->

Keo had heard it at Genesis, with only the three of them to hear it. Now the whole chamber took it in at once and went still. Somewhere behind him a woman began to cry, and stopped herself.

Hesh sat back.

"Two hundred and twenty from year three." He said each number by itself, slowly. "Two hundred and twenty-three. Seventeen." <!-- @WF-impact.window.max: 17y -->

He looked out over the floor, at the people sitting on it.

"At the most, he said. So not seventeen. Anything short of it. Ten. Two. This one."

His eyes went to the children in their parents' laps, and to the older ones standing at the back.

"Nearly every child in this room will be here for it."

Sila stood up.

Keo had watched her do that once before, from the back of this room, and had felt something rise in him with her. She got up the same way now, like someone just woken, with the whole room sunk around her. Nothing rose in him.

"Then we have seventeen years to be clean in." <!-- @WF-impact.window.max: 17y --> She turned to take in all of them. "There were two things we could not fix, and we did not add a third. This afternoon a third came over the barrier, and it is lying in our river, and every one of you has seen it. Whatever comes, it must find nothing of us."

Benal still had the floor.

"There's something else. What's in every child. The upgrades. I think they're the cure. It's what the founders came here to make."

The room had already gone back to the number. The voices came from all over the floor, over one another.

"Seventeen from when? From tonight?"

"From tonight," Hesh said. "At the most."

"My girl is four."

Nobody asked Benal to say it again.

Teva went past Keo onto the open floor below the dais before anyone had given it to her. Her fist was up and shaking at Sila, and her voice filled the chamber.

"He's telling you something and you're talking over him. You always talk over him."

Keo's face had gone hot, and he was with her.

"When Alira died, this room sat here and said wait." She swung round toward the side wall. "And the two best Explorers in the Enclave sat on that floor and said nothing —"

"Teva," Keo said.

"— with ten suits in the Repository that they kept for nobody. We went because you wouldn't. Because they wouldn't."

It was his own argument. He had made it to them in the nest on the morning of the debate, and had never heard it from outside. He did not like the sound of it.

"The custodians will answer for what they kept, child, and for what they lost," Sila said. "Two suits went out of the Repository, against every rule we have, on the most foolish plan this Council has ever heard, past every taboo the founders left us."

Heads nodded around the floor. A woman near the front had tears running down her face, and she was nodding with them.

"She's not a child," Keo said.

"Three children went out." His mother's voice came from the side wall. She did not raise it. "Three came home. That is what the suits are for."

She had said *children* once, and not the second time. Keo heard the gap and would not let himself believe it.

His father had not spoken. Keo turned his head and looked at him.

His father was looking at him already. It was the face from the back of this room on the night of the debate, nothing in it but fear. His eyes went down over the burns on Keo's arms, to the leg and the torn hands, and back up to his face. They did not go to the dais. Beside him his mother's face had not moved.

Standing still, Keo felt nineteen days <!-- @WF-story.span: 19d --> arrive in him at once: the burns tight on his skin, the rib catching at every breath, the cut in his thigh, and his knees wanting to give.

Across the floor, Marek sat in the crowd with Lunet beside him, and he was looking at Benal.

Benal stepped out toward the dais again, alone.

"The upgrades are the cure. What's in every child in this Enclave is what the founders came here to make. It's on their wall at Genesis, in their own words."

Sila's face lit.

"Then it is true. All of it." She came to the edge of the dais. "The founders put their work into our children's blood, and we have kept it there, clean, through every Seeding for two hundred years, as they told us to. Every rule we kept, we kept for this. The Mission was never lost. We were keeping it." She looked round the room. "Have I not said so? In this room?"

The room murmured, and some of it was yes. Keo was already moving.

"That isn't what he said," Lunet said.

"No," said Marek. He was on his feet. "It isn't."

"Marek —"

He did not stop for her. He spoke in the voice he used in this room, the one Keo had heard go up and up at the debate, and he walked out across the floor while he did it.

"I told my son his work was useless. Dead symbols, I called it, at my own table, more times than I can count. I was wrong. He is the best mind in this room, and he did what none of us would."

He stopped at Benal's side. Keo came up a step behind him and took the other. Back in the crowd, Lunet was crying.

Benal stood between them with the tablet against his chest.

"Nobody here doubts the boy, Marek," Hesh said. He looked past him at the floor. "Seventeen years. <!-- @WF-impact.window.max: 17y --> That is what this Council has to sit with, and it will not be settled in one night."

"What do we tell the yazhi?" someone called.

"Nothing, tonight."

At the side wall the watchers had not moved from his parents' shoulders.

"The children are home," Sila said, "and that is a mercy. The suits, and the custodians, are a matter for another sitting."

She sat again and looked at Benal, and her face was kind.

"Thank you, Benal. You must be very tired."
