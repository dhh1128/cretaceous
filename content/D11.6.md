Keo woke shaking.

It was dark. Water dripped close to his ear, and over him the rain hissed on the fronds. For a moment he did not know where he was. Then he had it: the overhang, the rock at his back, his leg, which smelled of the salve. He was hot all through, and then cold, and his teeth started to go.

He blinked, and he could not hear the rain. The drip by his ear had stopped too. His skin prickled, and the hairs lifted on his arms and the back of his neck, the way they did when a storm was about to break. There was a mark in front of his open eyes, bright and sharp-edged against the dark: a ring, with a straight line struck through it. Under the salve there was a smell like the air after lightning, and salt.

He blinked again and it was gone, and the rain was hissing on the fronds.

Fever, he thought.

At the mouth of the overhang, against the faint gray of the rain, there was a shape. It was thin, all knees and shoulders, and it looked as though the next gust would fold it up. It did not fold. After a while he knew it was Benal, sitting up with the razor-club across his knees and his face turned out toward the gulch.

"Ben."

"Here." Benal did not turn around.

"My watch. Wake me for the last one."

"Teva has the last one. With that ankle you'd be as much use on watch as one of the stakes." He seemed to think about it. "Less, possibly. The stakes don't shake."

"I'm not—"

"Go to sleep. I have this."

The rain went on. Keo had nearly gone when Benal said, "Probably."

Then Benal was coming back under the fronds on his knees, with a hand pressed flat to his chest. He had a skin.

"Drink first."

"Not thirsty."

Benal got a hand under Keo's head and lifted it. "You lost a lot of blood on the scree, and the fever's sweating you dry. Drink."

He held the skin to Keo's mouth. Keo swallowed, and choked, and swallowed again, and Benal waited until he had drunk a third time before he took it away. Then he laid Keo's head down, slowly, and went back on his knees to the stakes.

Teva was asleep at the back of the overhang. Keo could hear her breathing, long and even, under the rain.

Benal had begun to hum.

It was very low, pitched under the noise of the water so it would not carry back to her. It was Liaso's song, the long one. Keo had sung it across the savanna yesterday at the top of his voice, with Benal wheezing in late on the chorus. Now it came to him through the fever a little at a time, and he knew each turn of it before it came, and it came.

Keo lay and listened.

It rose in him then, from somewhere under the shaking, and it was so strong it hurt in his throat. It was for Ben, whom he had been kindly sorry for all his life, as everyone was. It was for Ben awake in the rain with a chest that would not let him breathe right, holding a skin to his mouth.

"Ben."

"Still here."

There was nothing he could say that would hold it, so he said nothing.

The song went around again to the beginning. He was going under, and he let it take him. The last he had was the shape at the mouth of the overhang, thin against the gray, still sitting up, still humming.
