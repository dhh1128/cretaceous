The sky came out all at once, thick with stars down to the trees on every side.

They had camped on a rise well back from the water, the gaps between the ginkgos fenced and gelled. Noli settled in a hollow beyond the gel, facing the river. Keo had the first watch. He sat with his back to a stake and his spike across his knees, the river black below him.

He reached up the bank.

The herd was there in the dark above the narrows, where it had come down to drink at dusk: a great slow crowd of warm presences, packed close. He could not pick one out. Somewhere in there was the one he would have to use tomorrow. He would not know which until he had it.

He did not push. Pushing cost, and tomorrow he would need all of it, to drive one out into the slow water, deeper than it wanted to go, its whole body telling it to get out and him telling it no, until the river had it.

He had pushed a wild one a little way, once, and paid for it. Never to its death.

The pressure was already there behind his eyes, as if he had been pushing all day. He ground the heel of his hand into his forehead, and it did not go.

Teva was awake. She sat a little way along the fence with her back to a ginkgo and her knees up, looking at the river.

He watched her a long time. She never once looked up, only at the water below them and the line of stones across it.

She was afraid of it, he thought. He had watched her stop dead thirty paces from the mud and turn back. She was making herself look at it all night, so that tomorrow it would be a thing she had already looked at.

"The seventh's up," he said. "Your field. I think. Over the trees."

"I know." She didn't look.

*We'll be fine* came up in him out of habit, and he swallowed it; she'd have heard it for what it was. "You should sleep."

"So should you."

"Nobody should sleep," Benal said from behind them. "Statistically. Somebody has to watch the river not do anything."

"Go to sleep, Ben. If anything comes, it'll want the delicious one first."

"That's why I'm awake."

"Moon tonight?" Keo said.

"Before dawn," Teva said. "Thin."

"There's honey left," Benal said. "In the skin."

"In the morning. Before."

"Before," Benal agreed.

After a while Benal lay down, breathing shallow over the bruise, too fast for sleep.

Once, far off, something slid into the river with a heavy splash. All three of them went still. Slowly they breathed again.

His body kept waiting for the water to move.

When his three hours ended, Teva was already sitting up. He passed her the spike and lay down. Above the narrows a branch cracked, and the herd shifted through his sense, one crowded mass. He shut his eyes and did not sleep.

Once before he had pushed at a croc with everything he had, in a flooded grove at home, with his sister beside him, and it had not turned its head. Tomorrow he would not be pushing at the crocs. He would be giving them something.

The sky grayed at the edges long before anything else. Then the river came out of the dark, pale and still, the stones standing in it.

Today they had to go into it.
