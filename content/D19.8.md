"Teva."

Sila said it from the center of the dais, and turned one open hand toward the floor below her, where Teva stood. The floor was hers.

She had the speech ready. She had made it at Genesis two days ago and been through it since, whenever her mind was free: three things, in order, level, with no tears in it for anyone to point at. It was an accusation from its first word, and she had checked every word of it against the founders' own metal.

For a week the anger had held her up. It had got her off the floor at Genesis and up the cut to the water, and all the way home. She reached for it now and found less of it than there had been. The chamber's stone had been drawing the heat out of her since they came in. Under the gray suit she had begun to shiver.

Here and there in the crowd somebody was still crying over the number, quietly, into a hand or a shoulder. Nobody near her was. The people nearest the open floor were looking at her suit. Elana stood at the front of them, looking at Teva's face.

Teva lifted her head to the dais and began, in the voice for the positions.

"Every time this Council sits, one of you stands up there and tells us—"

Hesh sat forward with his elbows on his knees and his hands hanging between them, and his eyes were on the floor at her feet. Sila sat very straight, with her jaw set. Around them the rest of the Council sat in the yellow-green light.

They were the faces from the water building.

Her mouth was still open on the next word. She had been afraid of this since Genesis without letting herself say what it was. Here it was. She stood still and waited for it to break her. It did not. Something under her breastbone let go instead, like a knot pulled loose, and her breath went out with it. Her own jaw was set. She could feel it.

The rest of the sentence did not come. Around her the room had stopped as well, the shifting and the low talk at the back gone all at once.

Up on the dais Sila waited, with her hands on her knees. The quiet went on, and the whole Enclave let it.

"Not liars… believers…"

It hardly carried past her own feet. She had not said it to the dais, or to anyone.

Behind her a voice was crying and talking through it.

"Seventeen years. <!-- @WF-impact.window.max: 17y --> Mine isn't even named yet."

The room took the number back up and went on without her.

After that the sitting came apart. People were on their feet and talking to each other instead of to the dais, and the ones who passed her went wide around her and looked at the suit.

Keo crossed the floor to the side wall. One of the watchers at his parents' shoulders stepped back half a pace to let him through. Teva had come a few steps after him without meaning to, near enough to hear.

Joram and Riel were looking at their son. Over Keo's shoulder she could see both their faces, and Joram's was working.

"It's everything you said it was. Out there." Keo's voice gave in the middle of it, and he pushed on. "You said I'd die in it, and I went without one, and I…"

"Came back," Joram said. It came out rough.

Keo stepped into his father's arms. Riel came in close against the two of them with her mouth by Keo's ear. She spoke low, and nobody else on the floor could have heard it but Teva.

"Two words. So efficient."

Riel's face did not move. The tears ran down it, and she did not wipe them. Joram had shut his eyes. Keo's back was shaking, and Teva could not tell whether he was laughing.

A few paces off, Marek still stood at Benal's side, looking at his son. Teva knew what his pity looked like, from Benal in the dark at Genesis: the hand on the shoulder, covering the whole of it, the waiting, and then the kindness. Marek's hands were at his sides. Behind them Lunet was crying with her fingers against her lips.

Benal turned. He looked at his father and met his eyes.

"You said a thing to me once, in passing. *Those aren't anything, Ben. You're just modeling a theory. What might be. Spend some time with something that you actually live.* That was the part I couldn't get, the part that makes the whole equation come out, and I h-had it from you the entire time."

The man who could talk this room down from the floor stood with his mouth a little open and let his son finish, and had nothing to say after.

Then Benal looked away from him, out over the heads, and she saw that he was looking for her.

She stood on the open floor below the dais, between the two families. She was the only one in the chamber watching both.

She had shouted at Keo's parents across this floor a little while ago, in front of everyone.

Her father had gone first. Her mother had gone two years ago, in their own nest, with the morning coming gray through the roof. Omya was on a stool against the Vitarium wall, with somebody else's loops in her hair, and this afternoon she had looked at Teva and asked about the figs.

She was glad. It went through her hot and fierce, for Keo with his face in his father's shoulder, and for Benal, whose father had let him finish. The other thing came up with it, out of the same place, and she could not have one without the other. There was nobody in this room for her.

The lightmold along the wall swam and doubled. Something ran warm down beside her nose and into the corner of her mouth, and it was salt. Her chin would not hold still. She kept her arms at her sides and did not put a hand up, and her face went on getting wet.

Across the floor Benal's eyes had found her. Through the water she could not tell whether he saw.

It was not far down the passage. She stayed where she was.

On the dais the Council sat on under the lightmold. Out past the cleft the wig lay in the river in the last of the light. Every so often someone in the crowd turned toward the cleft and then back, and what was to be done with it had not come up. Hesh had put his face in his hands. Beside him Sila sat as straight as before, with her jaw set, and looked out over all of them.
