The dark went gray first, and then the fern began to come out of it, a frond at a time, gray and then green and then gold along the tops where the light found them.

Keo sat inside the fence with his spike across his knees. He had had the last watch, as he always did, and it had been an easy one. The others were still asleep behind him. Benal was curled on his side with one long arm over his face, and Teva lay with her back to the dead fire and her suit in a heap beside her. Nothing had come near the thorns all night.

Noli was asleep in the fern a few steps outside the fence, on the east side, with her head down on her forelegs and her tail wrapped round. Every so often a feather on her back lifted and settled. He held her lightly in his head, and what came back down the bond was the slow warm nothing of an animal fast asleep and full of grounder.

He was stiff, his forearms tight with yesterday's burn, and he felt fine. He felt better than fine. They had come most of the way across the open country and nothing had touched them. Tomorrow, or the day after, there would be water, and then the coast.

The sun came up over the edge of the fern.

He looked straight at it, because that was where she was. There was nothing to see. There was a line of white fire along the world and a glare above it that filled his eyes, and when he squinted he could make out the dark lump of her in the fern, and that was all. He looked away and blinked green shapes out of his eyes.

Then the light went out of the sun.

It was a shadow, too big to be anything, and it came out of the glare low over the fern without a sound until it was on top of her. Then he heard it: a long tearing noise, like a sail ripping from end to end.

It came down beside her in one stride. He saw a beak close on her neck. Her feathers burst out from it, and then the whole shape heaved itself up off the ground with her hanging from it and was in the air again.

It had taken a second. It might have taken two.

Down the bond came a single flash of her terror, so hard and so bright it went through the back of his eyes. Then it thinned. It went thinner, and thinner still, and it went out. He could not tell whether she had died or only been carried too far for him to hold.

He did not understand any of it until it was already over.

Then he reached. He threw everything he had up after her, *drop, drop, come back, down, come back*, at a shape already high in the sky and going away into the sun. Nothing came back. There was nothing at the end of it to come back. He had pushed into an empty sky.

He was on his feet. He did not remember standing. The spike was in his hand, and there was nothing to throw it at.

The bond had gone quiet. It had never been quiet before, not once since he found her, not even when she slept. It was not like sleep; it was like a room he had walked into every day of his life and found the floor gone.

The sky was empty, and blue. The sun was up over the fern, and the fern was gold.

Behind him, Teva and Benal were only now sitting up.
