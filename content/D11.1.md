The dark faded, and then the fern began to come out of it, a frond at a time, gray and then green and then gold along the tops where the light touched.

Keo sat inside the fence with his spike across his knees. Benal was curled on his side with one long arm over his face, and Teva lay with her back to the dead fire and her suit in a heap.

Noli dozed in the fern a few steps outside the fence, on the east side, with her snout tucked under one feathered arm and her tail wrapped round. Every so often a feather on her back lifted and settled. He held her lightly in his head, and what came back down the bond was the slow warm nothing of an animal fast asleep and full of grounder.

He was stiff, his forearms tight with yesterday's burn, and he felt fine. He felt better than fine. They had come most of the way across the open country and nothing had touched them. Tomorrow, or the day after, there would be water, and then the coast.

The sun peeked at him around the edge of the fern. He looked straight at it, saw only a line of white fire and glare. He looked away and blinked green shapes out of his eyes.

Then the glare vanished. A huge shadow swooped down until it was on top of Noli. He saw a beak close on her neck. Her feathers burst out from it, and then the whole shape heaved itself up off the ground with her hanging from it.

The whole thing took one second, maybe two.

Down the bond came a single flash of her terror, so hard and so bright it went through the back of his eyes. Then it thinned. It went thinner, and thinner still, and it went out. He could not tell whether she had died or only been carried too far for him to hold.

He reached, mentally screamed *drop, drop, come back, down, come back* at the shape already high in the sky.

Nothing came back.

He was on his feet. The spike was in his hand, and there was nothing to throw it at.

The bond had gone quiet. It had never been quiet before, not once since he found her, not even when she slept. It was not like sleep; it was like a room he had walked into every day of his life and found the floor gone.

The sky was empty, and blue. The sun was up over the fern, and the fern was gold.

Behind him, Teva and Benal were only now sitting up.
