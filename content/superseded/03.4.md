Teva’s words hung in the air.

Keo stared at her, mind struggling. Benal? Benal, who spent his days staring at symbols Keo couldn’t understand? Benal, whose father pitied him?

"Teva, what are you talking—"

"You'll have me."

The voice was thin, unsteady. Keo and Teva spun.

Benal unfolded himself from the shadows near the ceratops hide. All awkward height and sharp angles, his pale skin looked almost blue in the drifting bioluminescence. He was, Keo saw with a jolt, shaking so hard his teeth chattered.

"Benal?" Keo said, voice tight with disbelief. "What are you doing here? Go back to your dwelling."

Instead, Benal took a step forward, hands clenched. "I... I heard you. I was... waiting. To see if..."

He trailed off and swallowed. His eyes darted past Keo's shoulder, toward the border tangle. "Is... is the Watcher... is Liaso on duty tonight?"

"Liaso? Maybe," Keo said, confused. "Why?"

"He's my cousin," Benal whispered. "He told me once... he said the ground outside breathes. He said he saw a T-Rex once. Just... standing. Watching him. For an hour. He said he..." Benal flashed his teeth. His eyes were wide. "I've never been outside. Not once. Just... just standing this close to the edge... my upgrades... I can smell the wet rot out there. I can hear... everything."

A trumpet echoed from the hadrosaur pens—a sound as familiar to Keo as his own heartbeat.

Benal yelped and staggered, slamming into a boulder. His eyes were wide.

Teva didn't move. Keo just watched. A knot of pity and impatience tightened in his chest. This was Benal. This was the boy Teva thought they needed.

"Benal, go home," Keo said. He tried to be kind, but it came out as a dismissal. "This is real. This is eighty kilometers of mud and crocs and things that will eat you before you can blink. This isn't... it's not math. It's not symbols."

Benal flinched. The panic in his eyes didn't fade, but something angry rose to meet it.

"And Genesis?" Benal's voice was still uneven, but sharp. "What will you do when you get there, Keo? Hit it with your spear? Will you, Teva? Will you use your magnetic sense to navigate a command prompt?"

He pushed off the rock, hands balling into fists.

"Genesis is a temporal archive. It's old-tech. It's data. It's symbols. It's the only thing I'm good at. You're... you're the delivery system. I'm the key."

He hitched a breath. The words, now aimed at Keo, felt meant for his father.

"My work isn't 'dead symbols'," Benal said. "It's temporal anchoring. It's drift mechanics. It's the reason our ancestors couldn't just jump back. It's the reason Sila and Hesh are wrong. They're... they're arguing about a lie. A noble lie, maybe, but... it's still a lie."

He vibrated like a wire pulled too tight, caught between terror and pride.

"You can get us there," Benal said, gaze locking with Keo's. "I'm the one who can make it work."

Keo stared.

He saw the terror in Benal's face, the sweat on his forehead, the repetitive swallowing, the tremor in his hands. Benal was, Keo realized, more afraid of the mudwalk than Keo could possibly comprehend. He was terrified of the dark, of the sounds, of the idea of the border tangle. He was probably terrified of them.

And he was here.

He was insisting on coming.

Keo felt a shock of... not pity. Awe.

He looked from Benal's shuddering resolve to Teva's rage-fueled certainty. His own bravado, his easy "you're not going alone," his morning mantra of "everything will be fine"... it all felt childish. Shallow. He'd agreed to go because he couldn't let Teva go. Teva was going because her grief left her no other path.

Benal was going because his intellect demanded it, even when every fragile part of his body screamed at him to run.

This was real courage. Courage Keo had never seen.

He gave a sharp nod. The decision—previously an impulse, a reflex of loyalty—settled in his gut. Heavy. Final. Real.

"Okay," Keo said. The awe remained in his voice. "Okay, Ben."

He turned to Teva, mind already shifting. His father's training—the logistical mind of an Explorer—took over.

"But we can't go like this," he said, words coming fast. "Eighty kilometers. You're right, we have upgrades. But... Teva, you'll overheat in the first savanna." He saw the flash of anger in her eyes but kept going. "And Benal..." He looked at Benal's trembling frame. "You'll be dead in two days. A stingburn, a fall... you're not built for it. Not like this."

Teva looked at him, her eyes questioning. The certainty of her plan hit its first, practical wall.

"We won't last," Keo said. The idea solidified, and his stomach clenched at the heresy. "Not without old-tech. We need suits."

The word hung in the air, a taboo.

Benal's eyes widened, his intellectual fury vanishing. "Suits? Keo, the... the repository. The inventory. Your parents... they're the custodians. Stealing a suit is..."

"I know," Keo cut him off. The adrenaline was back, now a reckless resolve. He thought about locks, about watcher patrols, about the repository's location. "I know whose trust I'm breaking."

He looked at Teva, then at Benal. Pact sealed.

"That's why we go tonight. Right now. Before anyone else decides to 'do something real'."
