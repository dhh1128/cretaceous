# The wig

**CANON.** The old-tech vehicle at Genesis that carries them home on Day 19. The action climax, and the largest act of heresy in the novel. Supersedes all references to a "skiff."

---

## 1. The word

**WIG** — wing-in-ground-effect. A 21st-century acronym worn down into an ordinary noun across 206 years, exactly the way *saropo* wore down from *sauropod*. On the closed list in `lingo.md`.

**Nobody in the Enclave has seen one.** They have the word because their ancestors had it, and it survives as a term with no referent — a word for a thing that is not there. When Benal says it at Genesis he is naming something he has only ever read about.

## 2. What it is

Real technology, and lookupable: a **ground-effect vehicle**, IMO **Type 2** — *"certified to temporarily increase its altitude to a limited height outside the influence of ground effect."* That regulatory category exists and it is built around exactly this problem. The nearest modern analog for scale and appearance is the **AirFish 8**.

**Form.** A wide, flat-bottomed hull like a shallow-draft powerboat. Low on either side, **stubby wings — very short, very wide, almost fins**, sitting close to the surface. At the back a **tall tail with the horizontal stabilizer mounted high**, which is not styling: a straight-wing GEV needs its horizontal surface up out of the disturbed air near the water. **The tall tail is the visual signature.**

**Four seats, in an enclosed cabin inside the hull**, low, behind a windscreen a meter or two above the surface. At cruise they are not looking down at the world. They are screaming across it at eye level, and obstacles arrive edge-on.

**Power.** The founders grew a **photosynthetic power membrane** on the wigs' dorsal surfaces and on the station's roofs: an engineered living film that makes electricity from light and feeds itself on light and water. It ran for some years after they left and is long dead <!-- @WF-wig.membrane: dead --> — salt crust, drought between storms, cyclone damage — and there is no time to regrow it. **What flies the wig now is a bank of nickel-iron cells** <!-- @WF-wig.power: nickel-iron -->: the founders' Edison cells, stored dry, and their premixed potassium-hydroxide electrolyte, sealed in polymer drums. Filled, connected in series and charged from the station's radioisotope generators, at the little they still give (`plan/milieu-brief.md` §3), a full charge takes two to three days <!-- @WF-wig.charge.days: 2-3 -->. The cells are heavy, roughly 25–30 Wh/kg and several hundred kilos of them, and the electrolyte burns skin. `[-]` Stored dry is why there is anything to charge after 206 years — a fuel-burning machine would be a sculpture. **Range on a full charge, by the training system's figure: about 120 km** <!-- @WF-wig.range.claimed: 120km -->, worked out from the cells' rated capacity as if they were new. **The cells hold less.** They are 206 years old, and old cells revived from dry storage hold less than their rating, lose much of what goes into them as heat, and give less again under heavy draw like the lift rotors'. Nobody knows this until they are in the air. The flight home is about 88 km, the eighty kilometers <!-- @WF-route.overland: 80km --> overland and Genesis five to eight kilometers offshore (`plan/milieu-brief.md` §3); the trio still say *eighty*, because that is the walk.

**Keo also carries home what the membrane needs to come back**: dried starter cysts of the membrane organism sealed in glass ampoules, whose revival is uncertain, and an aluminum plate etched with its recipe and growing method, so that the Enclave's Vitarium can grow it on the wig in book 2.

**Two propulsion systems, and the difference between them is the whole climax.**

- **Cruise:** a main propulsor at the back. Efficient in ground effect, roughly a meter up, where the compressed air beneath does most of the lifting for free.
- **Lift rotors:** drone-derived units for vertical thrust. They allow a controlled lift off the bank instead of a long, rough planing run — which a 206-year-old hull would not survive. **They also drain the charge ruinously fast.** **They run off a founder buffer bank of supercapacitors, not off the cells.** The nickel-iron cells can deliver power only slowly, tens of kilowatts at most, and the four rotors need several hundred to hold a craft of roughly a tonne and a half up. So the cells refill the buffer slowly, minutes for each fill, and every fill costs range; the buffer empties in a burst, roughly ten seconds of full rotor thrust a fill. `[-]` On the page it is *the reserve*, the training system's word, never *the buffer*, which is the Enclave's patrolled grazing ground that D19.5 flies over (`plan/milieu-allocation.md` §5b).

**Ceiling: about fifty feet.** Above most of the canopy. **Not above the conifer emergents**, which stand at thirty meters — twice its limit. So the flight home is not "fly over the forest." It is threading **virtual canyons between the conifers**, hopping the low canopy, and going *around* anything tall.

## 3. The founder history, and why the wigs did not save them

The problem this has to solve: two hundred people <!-- @WF-firstwalk.departed: 200 --> spent most of a year surveying and then moving eighty kilometers <!-- @WF-route.overland: 80km --> on foot, lost a hundred and six across the surveys and the moves, and left aircraft behind. The answer is not that the aircraft were useless. **The aircraft worked, and were used, and could not solve the problem that killed them.**

**Four wigs came through.** Two survive at Genesis today.

**Phase 1 — Arrival and stabilization.** Transit went badly. Equipment damaged, people dead, and a long stretch of going nowhere at all: securing the bank, treating casualties, working out what still functioned. Genesis is defensible against land predators precisely because it is offshore, and it is hopeless for fresh water. That is the trade they lived on while they recovered.

**Phase 2 — Survey, and the wigs did this.** They had drones and they had four wigs, and they used both to look at the coast and inland. **This is how the Enclave site was found.** Aerial survey is exactly what a four-seat craft with `[-]` about 120 km <!-- @WF-wig.range.claimed: 120km --> of range on new cells is for.

**Phase 3 — The sky turned out to belong to something else.** Drones were taken out of the air. **Two wigs were lost, at least one of them to a flybeak** — a ten-meter wingspan aerial predator against a slow, loud, low-flying machine is not a contest. The founders learned that flying here is survivable rather than safe, and they learned it expensively.

*This is worth having in the records, because it makes the Day 19 chase a repetition of a founder-era disaster rather than a novel threat. Benal or the AI can know that flying kills people here, because it already has.*

**Phase 4 — The decision, and then the walk.** Having found the site, they had to *move to it* — and this is the whole answer. **A four-seat craft cannot relocate a colony.** Two hundred people <!-- @WF-firstwalk.departed: 200 -->, the cultures, the equipment, the suits, everything they owned. You could in principle shuttle it in four-seat hops over months, with two degrading airframes, `[-]` about 120 km <!-- @WF-wig.range.claimed: 120km --> of range, a charge cycle, and predators learning the route — while everyone left behind waits at a site with no fresh water. They walked instead, in moves across most of the year, and with the surveys it cost them a hundred and six.

**So: the wigs solved the finding problem and could not solve the moving problem.** Survey is not transport.

**Phase 5 — And then the wigs stopped being a resource and became a sin.** They were at Genesis; the colony was now eighty kilometers <!-- @WF-route.overland: 80km --> inland; retrieving them meant repeating the journey that had killed half of them. The remaining airframes had already been stripped for the move. And within a generation the hard-tech taboo hardened, so going back for them shifted from *difficult* to *unthinkable*, and **nobody noticed the transition.** Same drift as everything else in this book.

### The inversion

The trio do not need to move a colony. They need to move **three people and a satchel.**

**The wig is exactly the right size for the thing the founders could not use it for.** And they know precisely where they are going, because they walked it — knowledge only someone who made the journey on foot could possibly have.

**So the walk was not wasted. The walk is what makes the flight possible.** Nobody has to say so.

## 4. The two hulls

They find **two** wigs, and neither flies.

- **The dry one** — hull sound, structure intact, sitting where it was left: sealed for two centuries in a hangar on the hard, the same aluminum-skinned rigid-wall kit as the lab modules but long and high enough for a hull, banked to the eaves and sealed, its doors facing the ramp and opened by the same covered manual release as the hatch. From outside it is one more banked mound. The hull rests inside on a wheeled launch cradle that still rolls, and in D19.1 they push it out onto the level concrete before the lift-off (Q-CTEM). Its membrane is dead, as both are, and its lift rotor units are not on it: they are in dry storage with the nickel-iron cells.
- **The wet one** — settled in shallow water off the bank, hull ruined by two centuries of sea. It went down level, so part of it stands above the waterline. Its tall tail, crusted white, is the only fin anyone can see, from the crown's top, and it is what Keo walks to at dusk on Day 16 (Q-CTEM). **Two centuries in seawater leave nothing on it usable, and it stays a wreck.**

**The rotors come from dry storage.** The side that lost the argument about leaving took the dry hull's rotor units off and stored them dry with the nickel-iron cells, as they cut the plates and stowed the boats, so that the way back stayed open (`plan/milieu-brief.md` §3). **The job is a refit, not a fabrication**, which is what makes three untrained teenagers believable: the stored units fitted back onto the dry hull, under the training system's instructions. No machining, no electronics, no expertise — just instructions, leverage, and hours. Heavy and awkward, and it gives all three of them different work.

**The charge comes first, and it is Keo's alone.** On the evening of Day 16 (D16.4) the training system tells him it cannot fly without power, tells him about the cells and the drums and how long a charge takes, and talks him through filling the cells, some seventy or eighty of them, by hand with the caustic electrolyte, wiring them in series, and starting the charge on the whole bank, the first hours of which only form the dry-stored cells. `[-]` By D18.1 it has been charging for about a day and a half, and at D18.2 it becomes the way home: his training, the weight, and finding and checking the stored rotors, which D19.1 fits `[-]`. **Keo strips the wig to save weight.** It has four seats and three fly, so the fourth seat goes, and so does anything else not needed to get home: `[-]` the cabin's lining panels, `[-]` the fourth seat's harness and stowage locker. The charge still will not be full by noon on Day 19.

## 5. The AI, and it should be Keo

Not a general intelligence and not an oracle. **It is the wig's maintenance and flight-training system** — a pilot's assistant, which is plausible 21st-century equipment, knows exactly what the plot needs it to know, and knows nothing else.

**Hard limits, because an AI that knows everything defuses the entire mystery layer:**

- It knows **the machines**. It does not know the mission, the timeline, the schism, or that the upgrades are the cure. **Those stay Benal's discoveries.**
- It cannot repair anything itself, cannot leave, and cannot fly the wig for them.
- After 206 years it is degraded, and it should be **slightly wrong** about some things.

**Keo activates it, and the reason is not convenience.**

Benal is the obvious choice, which is why he is the wrong one — he is in the temporal lab having his own vindication at D17.2, and giving him this as well concentrates everything on one character. **Keo has nothing to do.** That has been his condition since Day 11: useless, a burden, waiting. So he wanders off, on the evening of Day 16 (D16.4), and finds something to talk to. He consults it from then on.

And thematically it is his: **Keo is the one who talks to minds.** Proximity sense and projection are his whole gift; he spent the novel bonded to an animal that way and lost her at the midpoint, and he has been alone in his own head ever since. The boy who talks to minds meets a mind that is not alive, and it answers.

It also solves the pilot problem cleanly. **He is the pilot because the training system trains him** — that is literally its function — and because it would much prefer a qualified pilot and does not have one, which is a good character note for a machine.

## 6. Who does what in the air

**Keo flies.** **Teva navigates**, in the seat beside him, reading the line through the conifers — and this is where she gets back what she lost offshore at D14.2, in a form that matters more than it ever did on foot. **Benal is behind them with the salvage**, doing arithmetic on charge, distance and daylight, and saying numbers nobody wants.

Three jobs, three people, one machine. Nobody is a passenger.

## 7. The flight, and what makes it frightening

**It flies by day**, and the deadline is the battery rather than the clock. The range is whatever the old cells will give, which is less than the training system says and more than nothing, so what makes the flight possible at all is the days it spent on the generators at Genesis. They lift at noon and put eighty kilometers <!-- @WF-route.overland: 80km --> behind them in about two hours. They cannot fly at night, and a wig that runs out short is down in a flooded plain.

**They leave a little short of full, believing they have margin.** The charge Keo began on the evening of Day 16 takes two to three days <!-- @WF-wig.charge.days: 2-3 --> at the generators' rate and comes full around noon on Day 19, which is the day the crown's water runs out. On the training system's figure of about 120 km for a full charge, Benal's arithmetic gives them about a quarter to spare over the flight home, so the argument on Days 18 and 19 is not whether a partial charge will reach but how much margin is enough. They watch it climb, ration the water, and at noon `[-]` the thirst settles it: they lift at about 90%, believing they have about 108 km, and leaving feels a little generous, not much.

**In the air the charge falls faster than the kilometers.** The old cells give less than the training system's figure, the climbs cost more than anyone planned, in the chase and on the rotors at the tangle, and Keo's arithmetic under the flybeak is done against a number that keeps coming out worse. This is the training system's last and worst way of being slightly wrong (§5). They reach the river with almost nothing left.

**Ground effect is cheap; altitude is expensive; hovering is ruinous.** Cruise a meter off the flood and the air does the work. Climb and it costs. Use the lift rotors and it costs enormously — so the rotors are **hoarded from the moment they launch**. **Most of any climb is made the way real ground-effect craft make it**, trading speed for height in a short hop, with the rotors adding a burst at the top, and a burst is one fill of the buffer (§2). The lift off the hard is one burst. In the chase Keo cannot climb twice in quick succession, because the buffer is minutes from full again, and the flybeak can exploit that. Over the tangle at the Enclave he needs one full buffer, so he must have kept it full through the chase.

**The river is the runway.** The cyclone left broad water only in the lows, and their own river, the one they crossed at the narrows on Day 8 and whose bend runs through the Enclave, has spread to a sheet of water a few kilometers wide all the way from home to the sea — see `plan/journey-calendar.md` §1, §2 — and that is precisely the surface this machine wants. **The storm that closed the walking route opened the flying one.** Teva's heading from Genesis brings them to the river's mouth, and they fly home up it: Keo takes the flood's middle and cuts the bends over water, and zags onto land only round an obstacle. Ground effect is strong over water, weaker over wet flat ground, and gone over fern and scrub, so a land leg is a hop that trades speed for height and may cost the reserve. The river line is longer than the straight line of about 88 km, even with the bends cut, and the training system's figure never counted that. The flood is not a lake: woodland stands out of it, conifers stand far above it, and saropo herds stand in it.

**Obstacles, in order of how they must be handled:**

| obstacle | height | answer |
|---|---|---|
| the river's spread, and broad water in the lows | — | cruise, a meter up, cheap |
| wet flat ground | — | cruise, dearer, the cushion weaker |
| low canopy | ~10 m | hop it, and pay in speed |
| **saropo herd** | 8–10 m and moving | clearable, barely — but a herd is a wall that shifts, and a startled one rears |
| **conifer emergents** | **30 m** | **cannot be cleared. Go around.** This is the canyon system Teva has to read at speed |
| land, at a zag | — | a hop, out of ground effect; where the flybeak gains, since it soars on the warm air over land and must flap over water |
| **torosaurus**, a mother and calf belly-deep | — | not an obstacle to the wig: at the last zag she rears at the flybeak's dive, and it pulls out and gives up the chase |

**The chase is a resource crisis, not a stunt.** The flybeak forces them to climb; climbing bleeds speed; bleeding speed means sinking; below is water with things in it. Every evasion makes the next one harder, and the charge, the altitude and the airspeed are one number being spent three ways. **Keo's redemption is arithmetic under pressure rather than courage** — he is not braver than he was at the midpoint, he is *thinking*, with a machine, about a problem mental projection cannot touch. **He outlasts it, and nobody fights it**: he keeps to the water, where it must flap, until it is tired, and its last dive is turned by a torosaurus rearing over her calf.

### The last obstacle is the best one

**The border-tangle is fifteen meters high.** The wig's ceiling is about fifty feet — **fifteen meters.** The final barrier of the entire novel is *exactly* as tall as their maximum, and it is a wall their own people built to keep the world out.

They arrive low on charge, in hard afternoon light, and they have been hoarding the lift rotors the whole way for precisely this. **That is what the rotors are for, and it is a Chekhov's gun established at launch and spent at the wall.**

**Guard against anticlimax.** The walk out took eleven days <!-- @WF-mudwalk.days: 11 -->, and the sea two more, and the return takes hours. That is only acceptable if the return is a *different* ordeal rather than a reward — faster, louder, wholly out of their control, and frightening precisely because they cannot stop.

## 7b. The landing, and where the wig lives afterward

**It clears the tangle by almost nothing** — fronds on the hull, everyone inside hearing it — and comes down **on the river**, settling onto its own boat hull and running out to a stop against the near shore. Substantially intact. It will fly again.

**That last part is deliberate and it is the point.** A wrecked wig is a story that ends. An intact, working wig is an object the colony now has to have an argument about, forever — and it is the centerpiece of book 2. **It cannot recharge or fly back to Genesis until it has been grown a new membrane**, from the cysts and the etched plate Keo carried home, and whether to grow it is the argument.

### Why he aims for the river specifically, and why it is the last turn of his arc

On Day 19 there is floodwater on either side of the channel and in every low, and most of it is easy. **The river channel is the hard target — narrow, fast, and full of the animals his culture is most afraid of.** It is also the only water that will still be there when the flood goes down. Everything else strands the machine on mud within a week.

**The AI tells him this, and he has to decide whether to believe it.** A machine his people call an abomination, telling him to put down in the water his people call death. Choosing to trust it is the heresy completed in a single act, and it is Keo doing arithmetic about three months from now while something is chasing him — which is exactly the competence his arc has been building toward, and the opposite of the boy who took no suit because he wanted to prove a point.

### Where it comes down

**A bend of the river runs through the Enclave** — see `plan/milieu-brief.md` §4. That is why there is a tangle to clear on the way in, and it is why there is water on the far side of it.

The reach inside the perimeter is barred at both ends: log pylons driven between boulders, a droppable pole grating, weirs, netting. **The wig comes over the tangle by almost nothing and settles onto the inside reach**, between the two barriers, heading upstream into the current. With the charge nearly gone it cannot hold there, and the current carries it back down the reach onto the downstream barrier, where it fetches up against the grating as flood debris does and heels for a few seconds as the water piles against it. The three climb the grating's poles onto the top of the barrier along its top, where the nets are worked from above, and walk along it to the bank without touching the water. **The wig stays pinned against the grating, substantially intact**, and getting it off is the colony's problem.

**The reach is one to two kilometers of water**, which is barely enough to launch from and never comfortable. **Every future flight starts as a nail-biter**, and that is correct — an aircraft with an easy margin is an aircraft that stops being frightening.

### The wig sits on the Enclave's own internal frontier

This is the richest consequence and it should never be spelled out.

**It has to live on water**, because that is where it lands and where it launches from. And the river inside the perimeter is not the safe part of the safe place — **it is the least safe part**, the reason for the gratings, the drills, the buddy rule, and Yara. So the most valuable object these people have ever owned sits in the one dangerous place inside their sanctuary. A wound in the middle of the settlement rather than a thing beyond the wall.

- Anyone who uses it goes to the water.
- Anyone who maintains it stands in croc range to do it.
- **It cannot be hidden**, because its membrane, once grown, makes its power from light and must sit in the open.
- **It cannot be moved inland**, because inland it is scrap.
- **And flying it means raising the grating** — deliberately opening the croc barrier, with a crew, every single time. Using the wig is not a decision about heresy. It is a decision to let the river in.

So it is visible to every person in the Enclave, permanently, floating on the water they have been forbidden to approach since they could walk. **Every yazhi in the village wants to look at it.** That is a tragedy waiting in book 2 and it needs no help from anyone.

*Water is death*, and the only future they have floats on it. That is the argument book 2 is about, and the object makes it without anyone speaking.

## 8. The heresy

At D2.11 Keo leaves a machete on a shelf because taking a single blade — bought with two lives — is unthinkable. @D2.11

**On Day 19 he flies home in the largest piece of forbidden technology his people have ever seen**, and sets it down in front of the Council. That is the arc measured in objects, and nobody needs to comment on it.

It also makes the colony unable to pretend. You can argue with what three teenagers *say*. You cannot argue with a machine sitting in the open — which is the object book 2 fights over.
